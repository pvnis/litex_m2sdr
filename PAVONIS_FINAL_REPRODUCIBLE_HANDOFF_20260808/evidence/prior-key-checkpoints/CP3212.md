# Checkpoint 3212: SR-period 5 ms OTA no-gain result

## Verdict

NO GAIN, with a formal duration-gate partial. Both traffic endpoints complete
and the RF path is healthy, but the core receiver duration exceeds the
analyzer's inclusive upper bound by `68 microseconds`. The measured and
duration-normalized UL values both fail to beat the accepted 10 ms result.

Retain `sr_period_ms=10` and stop the cadence sweep.

## Frozen change

The only policy/config difference from CP3209 was:

```yaml
pucch:
  sr_period_ms: 5
```

Baseline `min_k2=4`, 512-byte SR bootstrap, native SR/BSR, RF geometry, MCS,
bandwidth, duplex, spatial layers, and benchmark tooling were unchanged.

## Measurement

Both endpoints mark complete and the final receiver ACK arrived.

| Direction | CP3209 at 10 ms | CP3212 at 5 ms | Delta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| DL | 2.660295 Mbit/s | 1.898224 Mbit/s | -28.65% |
| UL | 0.187816 Mbit/s | 0.179182 Mbit/s | -4.60% |

The authoritative UL receiver ran `47.000069 s`, just `68 microseconds` beyond
the analyzer's accepted upper bound. Normalizing its exact byte count to
45 seconds gives `0.187146 Mbit/s`, still `0.36%` below CP3209. Therefore the
gate miss cannot conceal a throughput improvement.

## Scheduler evidence

- steady UL newTx rows: `6,771`;
- median positive newTx gap: `5` slots;
- median inter-burst gap: `5` slots;
- burst-size median/p90: `1/2`;
- mean/median TBS: `501.10/512 bytes`;
- all grants: `k2=4`.

Five milliseconds clearly creates more frequent scheduling opportunities, but
the delivered bytes do not rise. Opportunity cadence is no longer the active
bottleneck below 10 ms.

## Radio health

- PUSCH CRC OK: `76/128`;
- median CRC-OK PUSCH SINR: `7.407 dB`;
- median PUSCH TBS: `528 bytes`;
- Soapy TX timeout/downlink-late: `0/0`.
