## Interpretation

This checkpoint separates two issues:

- The CP43 no-Msg1 behavior was caused or strongly perturbed by excessive RF timestamp probing in the TX hot path. After selective probing, the UE again reached usable PRACH config and produced high-peak Msg1 buffers.
- The original Stage 6 problem remains: valid full-scale Msg1 buffers are accepted by `bladerf_sync_tx()` with OK status, but the gNB/M2SDR PRACH windows still see no corresponding RF energy.

The high-peak writes are not obviously late at the write boundary: the requested timestamps are about `1.47 ms` to `2.22 ms` ahead of the sampled RX timestamp. The suspicious remaining timing fact is the first timed TX SOB being scheduled about `0.81 ms` behind the sampled RX timestamp. A late initial SOB may corrupt the later continuous timed TX stream even when later continuation writes are future queued and return OK.

## Next Step

Run a controlled plugin test that mimics the live UE's late initial timed SOB, while replaying a known-good CP33/CP45 PRACH waveform through the same bladeRF TX path. If that controlled late-SOB replay fails at the gNB while the earlier future-SOB sparse replay passed, the first stream-start timestamp is the likely failure mechanism. If it still radiates cleanly, the failure is more specific to the live srsUE continuous TX state or absolute timing alignment.
