## Interpretation

CP64 proves three things:

1. Guard=30000 can reach Msg1 on the current CP57 binary. CP57/CP58 no-PRACH should not be treated as deterministic guard=30000 behavior.
2. Guard=30000 is better than guard=24000 for the local `-14` symptom in this run, but not sufficient.
3. `-14` can still occur even when the requested timestamp is exactly 30000 samples ahead of the sampled TX timestamp.

The sampled-TX-timestamp guard model is therefore incomplete. The effective bladeRF queued/burst boundary can still be later than `bladerf_get_timestamp(BLADERF_TX) + 30000`, or the `-14` is tied to burst/stream state rather than only lead time.

The gNB problem is also unchanged. None of CP60, CP62, CP63, or CP64 produced accepted PRACH at the gNB.

## Next Experiment

The next timing checkpoint should avoid more small guard increments unless we are specifically mapping the `-14` threshold. Two viable options:

- CP65A: one guard=40000 run to see whether a much larger guard finally eliminates `-14`.
- CP65B: change strategy and schedule the forced SOB burst later by one PRACH period or insert a deliberate EOB-only reset before each timed SOB|EOB burst, because the current lead guard does not fully model the bladeRF TX queue.

If continuing the ladder mechanically, CP65A is the narrower experiment.
