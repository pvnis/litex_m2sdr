## Interpretation

CP86 does not support a simple overheating explanation for the passive antenna or for either SDR's RFIC:

- [host-redacted] bladeRF RFIC was `36 C`.
- [host-redacted] AD9361 was `39 C`.
- [host-redacted] M2SDR FPGA was warmer at about `64 C`, but not obviously at a shutdown/fault threshold in this snapshot.
- Both host CPUs were in normal operating ranges.

The more important unresolved findings are:

- CP83 already showed elevated no-TX RF energy at the UL center.
- CP82-CP85 show the historical CP45 known-good replay is currently degraded.
- Repeated bladeRF USB resets appeared in the [host-redacted] kernel log during the checkpoint period.
- [host-redacted] has duplicate Soapy module load warnings and a probe-time SPI warning, though the device still initializes.
- No stale process was found on either host.

The current evidence points more strongly at RF environment/interference, physical RF path state, or lower-level SDR/device state than at antenna overheating. A passive antenna overheating remains unlikely. If there is an active element in the path, an external amplifier/LNA, powered antenna, connector, attenuator, or cable problem could still be plausible, but CP86 did not directly instrument those external components.
