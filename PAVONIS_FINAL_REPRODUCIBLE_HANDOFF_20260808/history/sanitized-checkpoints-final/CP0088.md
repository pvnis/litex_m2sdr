## Interpretation

This establishes that:

- The current transmitter, receiver, harness, and analyzer can still produce a clean CP48-style controlled replay.
- The degraded CP82-CP85 results were not caused by a permanently broken CP45 waveform, bladeRF TX path, or local sparse detector.
- The receiver-side setting change to [host-redacted] RX gain `30` plus bandwidth `3 MHz` is sufficient to recover known-good replay quality under current conditions.

The controlled RF path is usable again for dump-level comparisons, as long as the [host-redacted] capture stays at RX gain `30` and bandwidth `3 MHz`.

Run the CP79 dump first with baseline replay flags, not CP79 burst-only/guard/offset flags, so the result isolates the waveform content from the experimental CP79 TX scheduling hooks. If it remains weak while CP45 is clean, then the CP79 dump content or generation context is suspect.
