## Interpretation

CP90 proves that the CP79 RF hook environment does not prevent the waveform from being transmitted or recovered by sparse template correlation. Under the recovered receiver settings, the CP79-env replay is strong:

- CP80 CP79-env at RX gain 40, BW 10 MHz: `3/7` selected windows >= `0.50`
- CP89 CP79 baseline at RX gain 30, BW 3 MHz: `7/7`
- CP90 CP79-env at RX gain 30, BW 3 MHz: `7/7`

However, CP90 is not identical to CP89 in detector space:

- CP89 baseline had nominal OCUDU `s0/rb8/p0` hits for all seven selected windows.
- CP90 CP79-env had nominal OCUDU `s0/rb8/p0` hits for only four of seven selected windows.
- The first three selected windows instead had strong `rb12` hits.

So the CP79 hook combination is cleared for RF energy and waveform correlation, but not fully cleared for nominal OCUDU interpretation. The guard/offset/SOB/EOB combination may be changing burst placement or capture-window alignment enough to alter the detector sweep result even though the waveform remains strong.

## Next Action

Before returning to live UE/gNB, isolate which CP79 hook causes the OCUDU nominal-RB difference. The next checkpoint should replay the CP79 dump with only the burst-only/SOB/EOB behavior active:

- `PAVONIS_RF_BLADE_PRACH_BURST_ONLY=1`
- `PAVONIS_RF_BLADE_HIGH_PEAK_EOB_THEN_SOB=0`
- no TX guard
- no PRACH timestamp offset
- no after-error skip/disable flags
- keep [host-redacted] RX gain `30` and bandwidth `3 MHz`

If that matches CP89 with `7/7` nominal `rb8` hits, then the guard/offset path is implicated. If it matches CP90 with only partial nominal `rb8`, then the burst-only/SOB/EOB stream-boundary behavior is implicated.
