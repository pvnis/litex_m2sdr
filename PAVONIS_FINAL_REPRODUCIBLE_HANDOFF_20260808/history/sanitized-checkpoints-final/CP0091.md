## Interpretation

CP91 keeps the RF energy and sparse waveform recovery excellent, but it reproduces the detector-space problem more strongly than CP90:

- CP89 baseline: `7/7` sparse, `7/7` nominal OCUDU `rb8`
- CP90 full CP79 env: `7/7` sparse, `4/7` nominal OCUDU `rb8`
- CP91 burst-only/SOB/EOB only: `7/7` sparse, `1/7` nominal OCUDU `rb8`

This implicates the burst-only/SOB/EOB stream-boundary behavior as the main cause of the nominal OCUDU shift. The TX guard and PRACH timestamp offset are not required to produce the effect; if anything, the full CP90 environment was less bad by this OCUDU metric than burst-only alone.

This does not mean the waveform disappears. The waveform is present and strong. The problem is that the detector's nominal `rb8` interpretation changes under the forced burst/SOB/EOB stream-boundary behavior.

## Next Action

Run one complementary controlled checkpoint with guard/offset active but burst-only/SOB/EOB disabled:

- no `PAVONIS_RF_BLADE_PRACH_BURST_ONLY`
- no `PAVONIS_RF_BLADE_HIGH_PEAK_EOB_THEN_SOB`
- `PAVONIS_RF_BLADE_TX_DBG_TX_GUARD_SAMPLES=40000`
- `PAVONIS_RF_BLADE_TX_DBG_PRACH_TIME_OFFSET_SAMPLES=-16000`
- keep [host-redacted] RX gain `30` and bandwidth `3 MHz`

If that behaves like CP89, the burst-only stream-boundary hook is isolated as the detector-space regression. Then the next live UE/gNB attempt should avoid forced burst-only/SOB/EOB and test timing correction through safer timestamp handling instead.
