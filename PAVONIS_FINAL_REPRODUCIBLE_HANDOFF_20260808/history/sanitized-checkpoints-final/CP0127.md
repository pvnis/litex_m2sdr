## Interpretation

CP127 weakens the hypothesis that the CP123 live hook environment alone caused the CP126 controlled replay degradation. Removing the CP123 hook variables improved the result from one to two strong nominal hits, but it did not restore the earlier stable CP102/CP103 pattern.

This keeps the current split:

- The path is not dead: CP126 and CP127 both produced real high-correlation, nominal RB8/preamble-0 detections after the live CP123 failure.
- The current CP123 replay behavior is intermittent or timing-sensitive: most selected repeats do not correlate strongly even in baseline mode.
- The live CP123 gNB raw corpus still did not contain the waveform at all, so the live attach failure remains more severe than the controlled replay degradation.

Thermal/antenna note: passive antenna overheating remains unlikely as the primary explanation because the same broad RF path still produced strong detections minutes after the live failure. Active RF thermal drift, contact intermittency, stream-state/timed-TX behavior, or current lab-path variability remain possible and need a targeted check.

## Next step

Run a repeat of the known-good CP99 controlled replay under the current hardware state. If CP99 still returns `7/7`, then the CP123 dump/replay timing or CP123 waveform conditions are the suspect. If CP99 is also degraded now, the focus should shift to current RF path, M2SDR/bladeRF state, cabling/antenna/contact, or thermal drift.
