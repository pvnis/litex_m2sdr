## Interpretation

CP129 makes a broad passive antenna or current RF-path failure less likely. The immediately preceding CP128 run replayed CP99 cleanly under the same current RF settings. CP129 then sent a CP123 dump with clean plugin status, but the capture did not correlate with the intended template, with CP123 dump `000001`, or with CP99 dump `000001`.

The failure now appears to follow CP123 dump/live-generation conditions rather than the general controlled replay path. The live CP123 failure remains consistent with this: CP123 produced accepted high-peak UE writes, but gNB raw windows did not contain a recognizable copy of those waveforms.

This still leaves several active-RF possibilities open, including stream-state/timed-TX behavior specific to the CP123 live-hook path, interaction with the CP115 patched bladeRF backend, or a CP123 waveform generation/format issue. But it does not fit a simple passive antenna overheating theory.

## Next step

Run one more immediate CP99 baseline replay after CP129. If CP99 remains clean after the CP129 failure, that further rules out a time-progressive RF/thermal path collapse. If CP99 degrades too, then the thermal/intermittent RF-path theory comes back into scope.
