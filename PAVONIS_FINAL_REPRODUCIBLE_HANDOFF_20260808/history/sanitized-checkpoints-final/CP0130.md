## Interpretation

CP130 partially reopens the active RF-chain intermittency/thermal-drift hypothesis, but it still does not support a simple passive antenna failure:

- CP99 still produced four very strong sparse detections after CP129.
- The path did not collapse completely.
- The result is degraded versus CP128's `7/7`, so current path variability, thermal drift, contact intermittency, or timed TX/RX scheduling variability are now plausible active-RF contributors.
- CP129 remains worse than CP130 and appears CP123-specific or CP123-triggered, because CP123 dump `000004` produced `0/7` where CP99 immediately afterward still produced `4/7`.

This suggests two overlapping issues may be present:

- a CP123/live-generation or CP123 replay-specific issue,
- plus current controlled-path intermittency that can reduce even known-good CP99 replay from `7/7` to `4/7`.

## Next step

The next diagnostic should either add an explicit cool-down and rerun CP99/CP123 pairs, or inspect physical/active RF conditions directly: SDR temperature, cable/connector seating, power rails, USB/PCIe errors, and whether the same selected bursts drop out in a time-correlated pattern.
