## Interpretation

CP152 did not reproduce the CP142 -> CP143 recovery pattern. Instead, the same controlled replay degraded from CP151's `5/7` to `3/7`, while the harness, TX source, waveform, gains, and return codes stayed clean.

This strengthens the active RF-chain/path variability hypothesis. The exact CP148 waveform is valid enough to produce strong controlled hits, but repeated controlled activity is not stable in the current state. The live srsUE path remains suspect for the CP148 raw-IQ absence, but CP152 means the broader RF path cannot be treated as fully healthy.

Thermal or state-related behavior is now more plausible than it was after CP143 alone, but the evidence still points to active components or SDR state rather than a passive antenna by itself:

- bladeRF controlled TX reports OK high-peak writes,
- [host-redacted] capture sometimes sees the waveform strongly,
- the hit count varies across immediate repeats,
- no process leftovers or harness errors explain the change.

## Next step

Take an RF host health/thermal/process snapshot now, before more transmit activity. After that, either do a short cooldown and repeat the same controlled replay, or run a lower-duty controlled check to separate thermal/path degradation from ordinary replay variability.
