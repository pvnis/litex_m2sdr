# Checkpoint 253 - Offline gNB predemod energy at target PRACH windows

## Summary

CP253 checked whether the gNB predemod captures show an energy bump at the UE PRACH target windows for CP245, CP247, and CP251.

Result: there is still no consistent receiver-side energy signature corresponding to the UE high-peak PRACH writes.

The strongest statement remains detector-side:

```text
CP245 accepted_sum=0
CP247 accepted_sum=0
CP251 accepted_sum=0
```

CP251 target windows 2 and 3 had high raw RMS percentiles, but their raw peaks were near the run floor and their detector peaks stayed ordinary. That is not a convincing PRACH-arrival signature.

## Method

For each UE high-peak RF write:

1. Estimate the RF target time from `sync_realtime_before_ns` plus `(timestamp - pre_tx_timestamp) / 11.52e6`.
2. Find the nearest gNB `pavonis_prach_predemod_manifest.tsv` row by `wall_ns`.
3. Join that row to the same-index `pavonis_prachdet_trace.log` row.
4. Compare `raw_rms`, `raw_peak`, `best_peak`, and `p0_peak` against the run-wide manifest/trace distributions.

This still uses wall-clock metadata, so target selection is approximate. The point is to look for a strong local energy signature, not to prove sample-exact timing.

## Interpretation

Across CP245, CP247, and CP251:

- the gNB never reports a non-empty `detected_preambles`,
- detector absolute peaks stay well below the threshold,
- target predemod windows do not show a consistent energy rise.

That shifts suspicion away from small UE timestamp-placement errors alone. The next useful work should verify one of the more fundamental interfaces:

- physical RF path and power from [host-redacted] TX to [host-redacted] RX,
- UE PRACH waveform/frequency placement against the gNB detector config,
- or whether the gNB predemod capture point is the right receiver-side observation point for the UE PRACH burst.
