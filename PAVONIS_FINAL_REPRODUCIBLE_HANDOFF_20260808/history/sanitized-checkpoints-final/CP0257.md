These do not support a simple "the passive antenna got hot and stopped working" explanation. There is also no direct antenna temperature sensor in the collected data.

## Evidence for active RF-chain/path variability

Several controlled replay checkpoints did show path-quality changes over time:

- CP82: historical CP45 known-good replay degraded to `4/7` strong sparse hits.
- CP87: no-TX receiver floor was elevated, especially at 10 MHz bandwidth, and was only weakly gain-dependent.
- CP88: changing [host-redacted] receive settings to RX gain `30` and bandwidth `3 MHz` restored CP45 known-good replay to `7/7`.
- CP132: after a five-minute idle interval, CP99 recovered from CP130's `4/7` to `7/7`.
- CP134: CP123 dump `000004` recovered from CP129's `0/7` to `7/7` after the recovered path.

That supports some kind of active RF/path variability: receiver noise/interference, analog front-end state, cable/contact intermittency, SDR stream/device state, or thermal drift in an active component.

## Evidence against a clean cooldown-only model

The cooldown pattern is not consistent:

- CP132 recovered after five minutes.
- CP154 did not recover after five minutes; it degraded from `5/7` to `3/7` to `2/7`.

So "let it cool for five minutes and it works" is not a reliable model. If temperature is involved, it is probably not a simple passive antenna temperature curve. It could still be an active component or contact/interconnect issue that sometimes recovers with idle time and sometimes does not.

## Most likely interpretation

Passive antenna overheating: unlikely as the primary explanation.

Active RF-chain/path instability: plausible and worth testing. This includes:

- bladeRF or M2SDR analog/device state
- USB/device resets or stream-state problems
- external RF interference/noise floor
- cable/connector/contact intermittency
- powered external RF components, if any exist in the bench path
- thermal drift in an active RF component rather than the passive antenna
