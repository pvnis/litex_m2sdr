## UE PRACH writes

Five PRACH dumps were produced:

- `pavonis_rf_blade_tx_dbg_000001.sc16` through `000005.sc16`
- each has matching `.meta.txt`
- each high-peak write returned `status=0`, `meta_status=0x0`, `flags=0x3`

PRACH TX rows:

| dump | tti_tx | cf32_peak | timestamp | old-pre_tx | final-pre_tx | final-old | post_tx_delta |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 000001 | 494 | 0.9991 | 56570869 | -114246 | 24000 | 138246 | 19051 |
| 000002 | 654 | 1.0054 | 58283949 | 15874 | 24000 | 8126 | 18550 |
| 000003 | 814 | 0.9910 | 60124188 | 18793 | 24000 | 5207 | 18800 |
| 000004 | 974 | 0.9926 | 61965054 | 21163 | 24000 | 2837 | 18173 |
| 000005 | 1134 | 0.9926 | 63815066 | 14345 | 24000 | 9655 | 18926 |

Interpretation of timestamp table:

- `old-pre_tx` is the original UE PRACH timestamp before RF guard/offset compared with pre-call TX device time.
- The first write was already in the past after the first-high-peak TX-stream restart.
- Later writes had only about 14k-21k samples of original TX lead.
- With guard 40000 and offset -16000, the RF driver rewrote every final timestamp to exactly `pre_tx + 24000`.
- `bladerf_sync_tx()` returned while the final timestamp was still about 18k-19k samples in the future, so the writes were not late after rewriting.
- The rewrite also moved each later PRACH by thousands of samples from the UE's original scheduled timestamp.

## Classification

`CP296_POSTSYNC_TS_WRITES_OK_BUT_GNB_NO_DETECT_RF_GUARD_SHIFTS_PRACH`

CP296 proves the UE bladeRF writes are accepted with positive post-call TX lead after the guard/offset rewrite, but gNB still detects no PRACH. The new evidence points at the RF-driver guard/offset rewrite moving live PRACH out of the gNB PRACH window rather than at RX gain, TX gain, or Soapy timeout failure.

## Next action

Run a native timed PRACH variant with:

- no RF timestamp guard
- no RF PRACH time offset
- no first-high-peak TX-stream restart

That preserves the UE's original PRACH timestamp and lets CP293 post-sync fields tell us whether native timed writes are accepted or fail late.
