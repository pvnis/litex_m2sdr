- `TONE_GATE_PASS=0`

The strict tone gate required:

- strongest peak within `10 kHz` of the requested tone
- peak-over-median at least `35 dB`
- exact expected-bin level at least `6 dB` over median

CP329 satisfied the first two conditions but missed the exact expected-bin
threshold by `0.63 dB`.

## Comparison to CP262

CP262 TX1 +100 kHz at the same center frequency, RX gain class, TX gain, and
tone amplitude reported:

- peak frequency about `+97.515 kHz`
- peak-over-median `55.18 dB`
- expected-bin level `10.26 dB`
- `RX_RMS_ALL=6.894e-03`
- `RX_RMS_MAX_20MS=2.078e-02`

CP329 reported:

- peak frequency `+97.207 kHz`
- peak-over-median `42.13 dB`
- expected-bin level `5.37 dB`
- `RX_RMS_ALL=5.721686874e-03`
- `RX_RMS_MAX_20MS=1.520631369e-02`

The gross TX1 path is still present and near the expected frequency, but the
margin is materially lower than CP262.

## Interpretation

CP329 does not show a dead or disconnected gross RF path. It does show a
weaker/marginal current path:

- the tone peak is close to the requested +100 kHz offset
- peak-over-median remains strong enough to be visible
- exact-bin margin is just below the strict gate
- the result is weaker than CP262 by roughly `13 dB` on peak-over-median and
  roughly `4.9 dB` on the expected-bin metric

This supports staying below live UE PRACH. The next useful rung is a controlled
margin check: repeat the tone or sweep gain to decide whether the path is merely
low-margin/variable or whether the receive/transmit chain is continuing to
degrade.
