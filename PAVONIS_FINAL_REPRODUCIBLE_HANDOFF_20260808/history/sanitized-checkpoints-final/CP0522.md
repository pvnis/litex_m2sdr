## Interpretation

CP522 strengthens the timing-window explanation and weakens gross RF/RX failure
as an explanation for the latest negatives. Raw CP361 is strong in all compared
runs, but PRACH predemod only improves when the high-peak replay lands within
about one millisecond of a detector window.

The failed CP518 result is also informative: `TIMED_LEAD_MS=0.701` should not be
treated as a simple PRACH-window translation knob. It changed the run into a
different timing regime where the nearest high-peak call was about `21.75 ms`
from a PRACH row.

Do not spend the next checkpoint on another blind RF timing rung. The next
useful step is to audit the replay timestamp math and CP515/CP518 high-peak TX
debug records around the relevant calls, especially CP515 calls `5197` and
`5356`, to determine which program knob can move the partial CP515 copy by
hundreds of microseconds without causing the multi-10-ms regime jump seen in
CP518 and CP520.
