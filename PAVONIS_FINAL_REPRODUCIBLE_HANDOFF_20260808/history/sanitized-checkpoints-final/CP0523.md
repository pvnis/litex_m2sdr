# Checkpoint 523 - Replay Timestamp Sweep Audit

Date: [date-redacted] UTC

## Purpose

Audit the replay source and TX debug records after CP522, without RF, to decide
whether the next step should be another timing run or more local analysis.

## Source Audit

The CP515 and CP518 replay source files are identical. The behavior is controlled
by runtime arguments.

Relevant source behavior:

- `TIMED_LEAD_MS` is added to `start_secs/start_frac` before the send loop.
- `FIRST_BURST_MS` is rounded to a whole 1 ms chunk:
  `first_burst_chunk = round(first_burst_ms / chunk_ms)`.
- `PERIOD_MS=159` becomes `period_chunks=159`.
- Each high-peak burst is therefore one 1 ms CP361 chunk every 159 ms.

This means `TIMED_LEAD_MS` shifts the whole scheduled stream start relative to
the replay program's current bladeRF time. It is not a direct knob for moving a
known CP515 PRACH-window partial inside the gNB's PRACH grid.

## Interpretation

CP515 was not simply "first burst 585 is always correct." It was the run where
the independent gNB PRACH grid and [host-redacted] replay schedule happened to place
the 159 ms sweep across zero during the 8 s capture.

CP518 and CP520 were valid raw-positive runs, but they started on the early side
of the PRACH phase and the short 47-burst sweep moved farther away from zero.
That explains why they stayed PRACH-negative despite strong raw CP361
visibility.

The next RF checkpoint should make one self-contained strict-gated run that
covers a full PRACH phase cycle, rather than trying another single offset. A
duration of about `32 s` with `PERIOD_MS=159` gives roughly 201 one-ms bursts,
enough to sweep more than `160 ms` of relative phase. Keep the CP515/CP520
readStream setup and strict [host-redacted] GO/CANCEL gate. This spends more TX than the
8 s rungs, but it replaces repeated blind rungs with one bounded phase-covering
test.
