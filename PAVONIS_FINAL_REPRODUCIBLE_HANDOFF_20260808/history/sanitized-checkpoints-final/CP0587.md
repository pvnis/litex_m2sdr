## Interpretation

CP587 is a valid no-accept but a positive timing result. Compared with CP585,
the best detector row moved from illegal delay `129` to legal delay `14`, while
remaining just below threshold (`0.2256` versus `0.24`). The exact same PRACH
window is also the top same-window CP570 correlation result (`0.24346`), and the
focused lower-Soapy analysis places the CP570 template within `303` samples of
the PRACH window boundaries.

This sharply narrows the remaining blocker. Timing and active PRACH extraction
are now good enough to put a coherent CP570-like copy in the detector window.
The remaining failure is detector strength/threshold, not gross RF visibility,
Soapy timeout state, SOB/EOB semantics, or the large timing offset.

The next targeted RF step should not be another large timing move. Two useful
next rungs are:

- lower the detector threshold slightly, for example to `0.22`, at the CP587
  offset to confirm the acceptance path trips when delay already passes; or
- keep threshold `0.24` and test a very small local timing/power bracket aimed
  at raising the `0.2256` peak above threshold without losing delay pass.
