## Cross-Run Evidence

Exact same active recipe at offset `1773655`:

- CP597: valid active accept, `2` accepted PRACH rows
- CP599: valid repeat, `0` accepted rows
- CP601: valid repeat, `0` accepted rows

The accepted CP597 rows had strong detector peaks and lower-RX copies close to
the PRACH windows:

- CP597 idx `40`: detector peak `0.9638404250`, same-window score
  `0.2410601228`, focused lower-RX score `0.9471026460`, implied full-template
  start/end `+983` samples (`+0.085330 ms`) relative to the PRACH window
- CP597 idx `199`: detector peak `0.8374090195`, same-window score
  `0.008823403157`, focused lower-RX score `0.9473748456`, implied
  full-template start/end `+618` samples (`+0.053646 ms`) relative to the PRACH
  window

The detector maxima in the two no-accept repeats still had strong lower-RX
copies, but the strong copies were much later relative to the PRACH windows:

- CP599 idx `211`: detector peak `0.1925374568`, same-window score
  `0.04432307556`, focused lower-RX score `0.9385743770`, implied full-template
  start/end `+8508` samples (`+0.738542 ms`) relative to the PRACH window
- CP601 idx `129`: detector peak `0.2124271542`, same-window score
  `0.01682306826`, focused lower-RX score `0.9432304505`, implied full-template
  start/end `+8609` samples (`+0.747309 ms`) relative to the PRACH window

CP593/CP595 already showed that changing the CP587 offset by one or two samples
does not smoothly move this alignment. Those nearby-offset negatives had strong
lower-RX copies that landed roughly `-6828` and `+6832` samples relative to
their PRACH windows. This argues against solving the problem with another tiny
offset nudge.

## Interpretation

The active RF path is not disappearing. CP599 and CP601 both had:

- strict gate pass
- zero Soapy TX timeouts
- 10 MHz RX/TX readbacks
- expected `BURST_COUNT=198`
- `RX_LOAD_ERRORS=0`
- strong CP570-shaped lower-RX copies near the detector maxima

The current failure mode is phase/alignment stochasticity between the replayed
CP570 burst and the active gNB PRACH detector windows. Offset `1773655` is a
real accept point, but it is not a deterministic recipe by itself.

The accepted and no-accept repeats separate most cleanly by the focused
lower-RX full-template offset:

- accepted: about `+0.05` to `+0.09 ms`
- no-accept detector maxima: about `+0.74` to `+0.75 ms`

That pattern is more consistent with run-start or burst-to-gNB-frame phase
alignment than with gross RF path loss, antenna overheating, or a missing TX
burst. If the antenna or RF path had faded out, the focused lower-RX scores
would not remain around `0.94`.

## Next Step

Do not spend the next live attempt on another same-offset repeat.

The next useful ladder step is a bounded no-RF/source audit of the active replay
scheduling path to identify the knob that controls burst-to-gNB-frame phase:

- where `FIRST_BURST_MS`, `PERIOD_MS`, `TIMED_LEAD_MS`, and
  `RF_ENV_TX_DBG_PRACH_TIME_OFFSET_SAMPLES` enter the TX timestamp calculation
- whether the arm file/gNB start timing creates a free-running phase between
  replay bursts and PRACH windows
- whether a small phase sweep should be done with `FIRST_BURST_MS` or
  `TIMED_LEAD_MS`, rather than more same-offset repeats

If a live CP604 is needed after the audit, it should test one concrete
phase-alignment hypothesis and keep the strict GO/CANCEL gate.
