## Interpretation

CP659 confirms that the bladeRF USB reset lines are reproducible without RF transmission. They appear once per bladeRF open/info iteration, while all `bladeRF-cli` calls succeed and the device remains present at SuperSpeed.

That changes the CP658 interpretation:

- The reset messages are real and frequent.
- They are not caused by sample TX, antenna heating, CPU overheating, or USB runtime autosuspend.
- By themselves, they are not enough to prove a CP656/CP657 failure, because a normal bladeRF open path may be issuing or causing a device reset before successful use.

The current strongest conclusion is therefore narrower: do not spend time on USB autosuspend sysfs writes yet, and do not treat the kernel reset line alone as proof of a broken TX path. The next RF rung should assume the TX device opens cleanly, but keep kernel reset context in reports.
