# CHECKPOINT 673 - CP667/CP671/CP672 no-extract comparison

## Scope

CP673 is a no-RF analysis checkpoint. It compares the parsed no-extract results
from:

- CP667: CP663 live-dump waveform rotated `+500 Hz`, controlled replay,
  accepted.
- CP671: same `+500 Hz` CP663 live-dump replay family after later live tests,
  no accept.
- CP672: older CP661/CP570 controlled replay baseline after CP671, accepted.

Goal: decide whether CP671 looked like a global RF/control-path failure, a
waveform-family problem, or a run-start/frame-phase/receiver-window effect.

This checkpoint cannot prove Stage 7. It is analysis of Stage 6 controlled
replay behavior.

## Interpretation

CP673 rejects the simplest "everything is now broken" interpretation of CP671.
CP672 accepted immediately afterward, so the controlled replay path and gross
UL RF path still work.

CP673 also weakens a pure gain/attenuation explanation for CP671:

- CP667 and CP671 had nearly identical nominal TX waveform, TX gain, TX offset,
  strict-gate state, bandwidth readbacks, and Soapy timeout counts.
- Their top-row RSSI was effectively the same (`-51.56 dB` vs `-51.30 dB`).
- Yet the coherent detector result dropped below threshold and the best delay
  moved by 19 samples.

The best current explanation is one of:

1. run-start/frame-phase sensitivity for the CP663 `+500 Hz` waveform family,
2. receiver-window alignment sensitivity,
3. transient RF/path state that affects coherent PRACH energy more than the
   coarse RSSI metric,
4. or a remaining waveform-family issue that only appears intermittently.

The CP661 baseline remains healthier than the CP663 `+500 Hz` live-dump family:

- CP672 top P0 peak was `0.3169560432`, stronger than CP667 and CP671.
- CP672 RSSI was also about `5 dB` stronger than both CP667 and CP671.

## Ladder status after CP673

Stage 6 live PRACH/Msg1 remains the first failed ladder stage. Stage 7 is not
proven.

CP673 supports one next RF checkpoint before returning to live UE:

- repeat the CP667-style `+500 Hz` controlled replay again as CP674.

If CP674 accepts, CP671 was likely transient/frame-phase/path-state sensitive.
If CP674 fails while CP661-style baseline still accepts, the CP663 `+500 Hz`
waveform family becomes the main suspect and should be compared against the
CP661 baseline at a deeper sample/slot level.
