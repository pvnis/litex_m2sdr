## Method

For each saved dump:

- read `pavonis_rf_blade_tx_dbg_*.sc16` and matching `.meta.txt` directly from
  the tarball;
- reconstruct CF32 using `scale = sc16_peak / cf32_peak`;
- compute sample count, RMS, peak, and energy;
- compute normalized complex correlation and phase versus each reference;
- record the corresponding `PAVONIS_SRSUE_PRACH_CFG` TTI and CFO from
  `srsue.stdout.log`.

The audit does not run the gNB detector. It is a waveform-ranking pass only.

## Interpretation

CP693 does not identify a clearly better CP684 replay candidate than
dump000001 for the CP680/CP667 controlled-replay path.

The strongest CP684 candidate against CP680 and CP667 is still dump000001, and
CP691 already showed that this dump does not accept under controlled replay:

```text
CP691 best/P0 peak: 0.1517668068
CP691 detector accepted rows: 0
CP691 gNB non-empty detected_preambles: 0
```

Dumps `000006`-`000008` are closest to the older CP570 accepted family, but
they are farther from both the CP680 accepted replay waveform and the CP667
`+500 Hz` accepted template than dump000001. They could be replayed later if
the CP570 family becomes the target again, but they are not the strongest next
candidate for reproducing CP680.

The CP684 no-accept problem is therefore unlikely to be solved by simply
choosing another saved CP684 waveform from this run. The better next rung is
detector-domain analysis of why CP684 dump000001 has high time-domain
correlation but low detector metric.

Likely next checks:

- root/DFT-start/frequency-offset sweep on CP684 dump000001;
- amplitude scaling sweep on CP684 dump000001;
- per-delay detector metric and concentration comparison against CP680;
- inspect whether the CP684 waveform phase/frequency structure creates the
  observed metric collapse despite high global correlation.
