# CHECKPOINT 694 - CP680/CP691 local detector sample-offset sweep

Date: [date-redacted] UTC

## Scope

CP694 is a no-RF detector-domain follow-up to CP691 and CP693.

Question:

```text
Does CP691 fail because the CP684 dump000001 waveform is intrinsically
undetectable, or because the PRACH energy lands in the wrong part of the
predemod/detector window?
```

The test runs the local OCUDU PRACH detector over selected saved predemod
windows from the accepted CP680 controlled replay and failed CP691 controlled
replay, sweeping artificial sample crop offsets and both nearby RB offsets.

This checkpoint does not transmit RF.

## Method

For each selected predemod window, the probe swept:

- `sample_offset = 0..1116`;
- `rb_offset = 7` and `rb_offset = 8`;
- the same detector implementation used by the local OCUDU build.

The nominal row is `sample_offset=0`. Nonzero offsets are artificial crops into
the saved predemod buffer. They identify whether a later part of the same
buffer can satisfy the detector, but they are not themselves a live gNB fix.

## Key observations

CP694 validates the local detector harness against the known CP680/CP691
behavior:

- CP680 idx155 at `rb_offset=8`, `sample_offset=0` accepts:
  - P0 peak `0.2946811616`;
  - delay `14`;
  - accepted count `1`.
- CP691 idx147 at `rb_offset=8`, `sample_offset=0` reproduces the live failure:
  - P0 peak `0.1517833322`;
  - delay `71`;
  - accepted count `0`.

The new result is that CP691 idx147 is not intrinsically undetectable:

- at `rb_offset=8`, it has `195` accepted crop offsets;
- the first accepted crop offsets are
  `847,848,849,850,856,857,858,859,865,866,867,868,874,875,876,877,883,884,885,886`;
- at `sample_offset=847`, it accepts with P0 peak `0.7613193393` and delay
  `104`;
- at `sample_offset=1116`, it still accepts with P0 peak `0.7312205434` and
  delay `90`.

The highest CP691 idx147 P0 row does not accept because it falls outside the
delay gate:

- `rb_offset=8`;
- `sample_offset=613`;
- P0 peak `4.4853243830`;
- delay `130`;
- accepted count `0`.

RB offset alone does not rescue CP691:

- CP691 idx147 at `rb_offset=7` never accepts in the sweep;
- only two `rb_offset=7` rows reach P0 peak `>= 0.24`, and both are outside
  the delay gate.

CP691 idx201 is not a promising alternate detector candidate in this sweep:

- no accepted rows for either RB offset;
- all peaks stay below the threshold.

## Interpretation

CP694 changes the active theory.

Before CP694, CP691 could still be explained as an intrinsically weak or invalid
CP684 waveform despite its high time-domain correlation to the accepted family.
CP694 weakens that theory: the exact CP691 best predemod window can produce
strong detector accepts when the detector input is cropped late by roughly
`847+` samples at `rb_offset=8`.

The stronger explanation is now a timing/window-alignment issue in the gNB-side
predemod path:

- the CP691 waveform is present in the saved predemod buffer;
- nominal detector input sees it too weakly or at the wrong delay;
- a late artificial crop recenters the same buffer enough to satisfy the
  threshold and delay gate;
- therefore the failure is unlikely to be solved by only increasing TX power,
  choosing `rb_offset=7`, or selecting CP684 dump000001 versus another CP684
  dump.

This is still a diagnostic result, not a patch. A live receiver cannot simply
use this CP694 crop without understanding where the predemod collection start,
symbol bridge, PRACH occasion timing, and detector delay gate disagree.

## Regressions and caveats

No new RF regression was introduced because CP694 is local/no-RF only.

Known caveats:

- the local linked detector emitted only main trace rows, so CP694 lacks
  `TOP3`/`TOP8`/`SPLIT` concentration diagnostics;
- nonzero sample offsets are artificial crops into saved buffers;
- CP694 does not prove whether the timing error originates in the UE burst
  schedule, Soapy RX capture timing, gNB PRACH window construction, or detector
  delay semantics.
