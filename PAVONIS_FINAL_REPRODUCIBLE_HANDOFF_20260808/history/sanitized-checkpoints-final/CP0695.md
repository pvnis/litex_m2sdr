## Interpretation

CP695 narrows the timing theory.

The CP691 failure is not visible as a coarse PRACH predemod manifest shift:

- selected CP680 and CP691 windows have identical length, RB offset, start
  phase, symbol metadata, and 160 ms cadence;
- CP691 idx147 is stronger than the accepted CP680 idx155 in raw RMS and raw
  peak;
- the gNB still rejects CP691 nominally because the detector metric remains
  below threshold.

The CP694 crop rescue is still real, but CP695 suggests it is probably not a
simple "the whole PRACH collection window starts 847 samples too early" bug.
It is more likely tied to how the OFDM PRACH demodulator and detector respond
when the input origin and sample span are changed: crop length, FFT/symbol
alignment, frequency-bin alignment, and the delay gate may be interacting.

CP680 idx156 also remains informative: it is a CP680 high-raw non-accept at
`rb_offset=8`, while CP694 showed it accepts at `rb_offset=7`. CP691 idx147 does
not accept at `rb_offset=7`, so RB offset alone is not the general fix, but the
detector is sensitive to demodulator alignment choices.

## Next rung

The next no-RF checkpoint should distinguish CP694's crop effect from a true
collection-window shift.

Recommended CP696:

- run detector variants on CP680 idx155 and CP691 idx147 using fixed-length
  transformations:
  - CP694-style crop-shorter input;
  - crop plus zero-pad to original length;
  - circular shift;
  - leading-zero delay / trailing crop;
- keep `rb_offset=8` and selected `rb_offset=7` controls;
- compare acceptance, P0 peak, detector delay, and original-buffer coordinate.

If only the shorter-input crop accepts, the issue is likely in demodulator
length/origin handling rather than a live collection-window offset. If
zero-padded or circular shifts also accept at the same effective coordinates,
then a real receiver-window timing adjustment becomes more plausible.
