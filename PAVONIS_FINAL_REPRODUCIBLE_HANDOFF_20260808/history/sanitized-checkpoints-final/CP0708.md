## Phase history

### CP1-CP75: instrumentation and first RF path checks

What did not work:

### CP76-CP160: replay health, cooldown, gain, and bandwidth

What we tried:

- RF health probes, no-TX floor checks, cooldown, RX gain changes, and
  bandwidth variants.

What worked:

- Controlled replay could be recovered under some settings.
- Lower RX gain and narrower bandwidth helped in earlier branches.

What did not work:

- These changes did not transfer to live UE Msg1 acceptance.
- Controlled replay health was variable, so every RF result had to be gated.

### CP161-CP257: timing, UL symbol dumps, and overheating theory

What we tried:

- UL symbol dump instrumentation, TX-now variants, high-peak-first runs,
  restart-stream tests, wallclock debug, delay variants, and DL reacquisition.

What worked:

- More precise timing and window evidence was captured.
- The project gained enough visibility to separate "UE transmitted" from
  "gNB accepted."

What did not work:

- Live Msg1 still failed.

Theory update:

- Passive antenna overheating remains unlikely as a primary cause. There is no
  direct temperature sensor evidence, and the later data points more toward
  active RF/stream/window state than passive antenna drift.

### CP258-CP324: operational hardening

What did not work:

### CP325-CP404: active replay and strict gates

What we tried:

- Strict gates, RX gain 50, active replay, qcore/gate audits, and targeted UL
  capture under active gNB conditions.

What worked:

- The RF path was not dead.
- Strong active lower-RX copies could appear.

What did not work:

- Active gNB behavior did not match standalone capture reliably.
- Similar nominal settings could produce different detector outcomes.

### CP405-CP514: Soapy RX visibility bug

What we tried:

- Soapy RX tap work, bandwidth checks, and active raw-copy diagnostics.

What worked:

- A real bug was found: LiteXM2SDR direct buffers are two-channel interleaved,
  while the zero-copy consumer assumed contiguous one-channel samples.
- `OCUDU_SOAPY_RX_USE_READSTREAM=1` recovered strong active raw copies.

What did not work:

- Fixing raw visibility did not by itself recover live Msg1.

### CP515-CP557: threshold diagnostics and first RA path proof

What we tried:

  overrides.

What worked:

- CP551 showed fixed replay can accept Msg1 and trigger RAR/Msg3 scheduling
  evidence.
- This proved the gNB RA path after accepted Msg1 is alive.

What did not work:

- Threshold lowering is diagnostic only.
- Fixed replay RAR/Msg3 is not live UE Stage 7.

### CP558-CP603: RAW512 branch and phase/window stochasticity

### CP604-CP652: detector tracing and `ra_scs` source bug

What did not work:

### CP653-CP684: `+500 Hz`, offset `1773617`, and more attempts

What we tried:

- Live UE `+500 Hz`, PRACH time offset `1773617`, TX gain 59, and later
  `cell_cfg.prach.preamble_trans_max: 20`.

What worked:

- Live-derived waveforms matched accepted families after `+500 Hz`.
- CP667 controlled replay of a corrected live dump accepted.
- CP680 controlled replay of the exact CP678 live dump accepted.
- Increasing `preamble_trans_max` increased live attempts from 5 to 15.

What did not work:

- CP678 live had 5 attempts and no accept.
- CP684/CP687 live had 15 attempts and no accept.

Theory update:

- "The UE waveform is malformed" weakened.
- "Only too few live attempts" weakened.

### CP686-CP699: CP684 dump audit and demod input-origin hypothesis

What we tried:

- CP684 controlled replay, no-extract waveform audit, local detector origin
  sweeps, timing geometry audit, source audit, and a guarded gNB demod input
  offset knob.

What worked:

- CP694 showed CP691 failed predemod data could be locally rescued by shifting
  detector input origin, with an accepted offset around `847`.
- CP695 showed the failed CP691 window was not simply lower power than CP680.
- CP696 showed the rescue came from moving the input origin, not merely
  shortening input length.
- CP698 added the guarded diagnostic knob:

```text
PAVONIS_PRACH_DEMOD_INPUT_OFFSET_SAMPLES
```

What did not work:

- CP691 controlled replay of CP684 dump000001 without the demod offset did not
  accept.

Theory update:

- The lead hypothesis became OCUDU PRACH demod input-origin sensitivity.

## Where we are now

The broad question is no longer:

```text
Can this waveform family ever be accepted?
```

The answer is yes, under controlled replay.

The current question is:

```text
Why does the live gNB receiver path not see a strong/correctly placed PRACH
copy when the UE is transmitting a waveform that matches accepted families?
```

CP704 showed the demod input-origin knob can rescue controlled replay. CP706
showed the same knob does not rescue live UE. CP707 showed the live TX debug
waveform is good, but nearest gNB windows remain weak.

## Recommended next rung

Stay no-RF for one more diagnostic checkpoint:

1. Select CP705 strongest detector/predemod windows:
   - global best rows from CP706;
   - nearest saved-TX rows from CP707.
2. Run a local detector origin/RB sweep on those windows, modeled on CP694.
3. Decide whether any CP705 live receiver window is locally recoverable.

If the CP705 receiver windows are not locally recoverable, the next RF rung
should change RF/receiver visibility directly rather than changing the UE
waveform again. Good candidates:

- receiver gain/path changes;
- TX/RX physical path, cabling, attenuation, or antenna placement change;
- controlled replay and live UE side-by-side capture under the same active gNB
  state;
- direct comparison of receiver raw amplitude and detector metrics under the
  same launch stack.

## Bottom line

We have proven controlled PRACH and proven a real gNB demod-origin fix, but
live UE Msg1 still does not accept. The latest evidence says the live UE is
generating the right kind of PRACH waveform; the gNB-side live receiver windows
are the weak link. The current branch should focus on live RF/receiver-window
visibility, with one more no-RF detector sweep of CP705 windows before spending
another RF run.
