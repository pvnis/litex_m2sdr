## Historical roll-up

### CP1-CP75: first instrumentation and RF checks

We built the basic checkpoint discipline, predemod dumps, manifests, RAW512
capture, controlled PRACH replay, SOB/EOB diagnostics, TX guards, and early
camping/sync checks.

Worked:

- Synthetic and controlled PRACH became observable.
- The live UE could camp or approach Random Access.
- The report/hash discipline started to make regressions traceable.

Did not work:

- Live Msg1 did not accept.
- Early launchers and wrappers had environment propagation and orchestration
  mistakes that had to be hardened later.

### CP76-CP160: replay health, cooldown, gain, and bandwidth

We tested RF health, no-TX floor, cooldown, RX gain, bandwidth, and replay
variants.

Worked:

- Some controlled replay paths recovered under changed gain, bandwidth, or idle
  conditions.
- No-TX floor and RF-health probes became useful lower-rung gates.

Did not work:

- Controlled replay recovery did not transfer to live UE Msg1 acceptance.
- Controlled replay variability made it clear that every live attempt needed
  lower-rung health evidence.

### CP161-CP257: timing, UL dumps, and overheating theory

We added UL symbol dumps, TX-now variants, high-peak-first runs, stream
restarts, wallclock debug, delay variants, and DL reacquisition checks.

Worked:

- We separated "UE emitted something" from "gNB accepted Msg1."
- Timing/window visibility improved.

Theory update:

- Passive antenna overheating weakened as the main theory. There was no direct
  antenna temperature evidence, and later evidence pointed more toward active
  RF-chain state, USB/stream state, receiver-window state, frequency treatment,
  or powered components.

### CP258-CP324: operational hardening

The laptop-to-NUC connection was unstable for part of the campaign, so we built
self-contained remote scripts, watchdogs, direct SSH recovery patterns, and
safer artifact collection.

Worked:

- Remote access recovered after timeout episodes.
- All-or-nothing remote scripts reduced wasted RF when the laptop link was
  unreliable.

Did not work:

- Operational stability did not itself recover live Msg1.

### CP325-CP404: active replay and strict gates

We added strict gNB gates, RX-gain experiments, active replay, qcore/gate
audits, and targeted UL capture under active gNB conditions.

Worked:

- The RF path was not simply dead.
- Strong lower-RX copies could appear under active gNB conditions.

Did not work:

- Active gNB behavior did not reliably match standalone capture.
- Similar nominal settings could produce different detector outcomes.

### CP405-CP514: Soapy RX visibility bug

We investigated Soapy RX taps, bandwidth readback, active raw copies, and
zero-copy versus `readStream()`.

Worked:

- Found a real LiteXM2SDR/Soapy bug: direct buffers were two-channel
  interleaved while the zero-copy consumer assumed contiguous one-channel
  samples.
- `OCUDU_SOAPY_RX_USE_READSTREAM=1` recovered strong active raw copies.

Did not work:

- Fixing raw visibility did not by itself recover live Msg1.

## Current decision point

The broad question is no longer:

```text
Can this PRACH waveform family ever be accepted?
```

The answer is yes. Controlled replay accepts and can trigger RAR/Msg3.

The current narrow question is:

```text
How should the live UE PRACH frequency rotation/sign/value be changed so that
the live emitted waveform matches the controlled replay waveform that accepted?
```

The direct but not-yet-proven translation is:

```text
current live CP745 PRACH_FREQ_ROT_HZ = 725
controlled replay accepted after extra correction = -375 Hz
directional live candidate = 725 - 375 = 350 Hz
```

Do not overclaim this as the next RF setting until the sign/code-path audit is
done. The live `PRACH_FREQ_ROT_HZ` implementation may not map one-to-one to the
offline replay correction.
