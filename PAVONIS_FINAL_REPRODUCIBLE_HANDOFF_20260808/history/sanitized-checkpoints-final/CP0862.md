## Comparison

CP860 used the same waveform with bladeRF bandwidth `5600000` and got best score `0.17414465401644427`. CP862 repeats the `5600000` case and gets `0.16869593182456807`, so the wide-band result is reproducible.

Narrowing bladeRF bandwidth improves the result:

- `5600000` BW: about `0.17`
- `1000000` BW: about `0.25`
- `500000` BW: about `0.27`
- `200000` BW: about `0.27`

But even the best narrow-band bladeRF result remains far below CP861 LiteX-only recovery of the same waveform:

- CP861 LiteX-only `none`: `0.7726436566224887`
- CP861 LiteX-only `phy`: `0.7892397880535511`
- CP861 LiteX-only `ad9361`: `0.7730781047646772`

## Interpretation

The bladeRF noise bandwidth is part of the failure. Reducing RX bandwidth lowers the no-TX floor by roughly `11.75 dB` from `5.6 MHz` to `200 kHz`, changes estimated SNR from about `-2.27 dB` to about `9.27 dB`, and improves the reference-correlation score from about `0.17` to about `0.27`.

However, `0.27` is still not a clean waveform recovery and is nowhere near the LiteX-only `~0.77-0.79` baseline. So the current boundary is not just excessive bladeRF bandwidth. The remaining likely area is bladeRF receive frontend behavior, physical coupling/cabling/antenna geometry, I/Q orientation/calibration, or signal level into bladeRF.

Further PBCH/gNB debugging should remain paused until this bladeRF receive path can cleanly recover the CP860 one-subcarrier probe or a still simpler modulated probe. A useful next low-rung step is a bladeRF level sweep at the best narrow bandwidth (`200 kHz` or `500 kHz`) to see whether increasing received signal power moves correlation toward the LiteX-only baseline or merely saturates/distorts.
