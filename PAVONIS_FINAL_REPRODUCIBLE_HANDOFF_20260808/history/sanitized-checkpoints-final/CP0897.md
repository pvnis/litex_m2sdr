## Interpretation

Requested bladeRF RX gain is not a no-op, even though the bladeRF overall gain
readback stays pinned at `60 dB`. The raw level rose from `0.0075` RMS at request
`20`, to `0.0180` at request `30`, to `0.0707` at request `42`, with no clipping.

Higher requested gain improves SNR and some coherence metrics. Requested gain
`42` is the strongest of these three short captures: best SNR, highest FFT-peak
mean coherence, and highest autocorr coherence.

It does not restore the path to LiteX-like coherence. The expected-tone coherent
mean remains near zero for every bladeRF gain setting, phase-fit RMS remains
above `1000 deg`, and autocorr coherence remains far below the CP896 LiteX-only
baseline. This keeps the failure isolated to bladeRF receive behavior and/or the
LiteX-to-bladeRF physical RF path, not the deterministic file or LiteX TX/RX
software path.

The fresh gain-42 run also shifted the global tone peak to about `502.657 kHz`,
whereas CP895 gain-42 at the same `2 MHz` RX bandwidth saw about `505.058 kHz`.
That run-to-run frequency-offset change is another sign that the bladeRF-side
path is not behaving as a stable coherent receiver over these tests.
