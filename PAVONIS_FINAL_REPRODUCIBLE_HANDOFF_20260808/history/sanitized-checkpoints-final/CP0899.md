rather than on the older `+60 kHz` one-subcarrier probe. This tests whether the
current continuous-phase failure is caused by the bladeRF helper's manual-gain
state, AGC behavior, or stale no-gain state.

## Interpretation

Manual gain is not a hidden misconfiguration; it is the least-bad receive mode for
the deterministic tone. The forced helper proves that manual gain becomes
`42 dB` after RX enable, and its capture matches the CP897/CP898 gain-42 class:
good spectral SNR, stable-ish FFT bin tracking, but poor continuous phase.

AGC and no-gain are worse. They leave AGC enabled, push the signal close to the
SC16 rail, reduce tone SNR to about `20 dB`, and make window-frequency tracking
spread across roughly `11-12 kHz`. They do not recover coherence.

This closes the gain-mode branch for the current tone path. The remaining useful
branches are bladeRF receive correction/calibration/retune behavior and the
physical RF path. The next RF action should avoid AGC/no-gain and keep manual
gain at `42` unless testing a specific reset or calibration hypothesis.
