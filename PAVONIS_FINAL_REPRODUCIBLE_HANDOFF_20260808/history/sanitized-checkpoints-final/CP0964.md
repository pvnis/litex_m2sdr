## Interpretation

CP964 proves that the external path can deliver much stronger received energy without digital clipping:

- SSB power rose from CP962's `+4.10 dB` over no-TX to `+12.60 dB`.
- Peak SNR rose from `16.18 dB` to `25.49 dB`.
- Raw max rose to `1807`, still below the `[date-redacted]` clipping estimate threshold.

The waveform recovery improved only modestly:

- CP962 x4 best normal score: `0.1960413357`
- CP964 x16 best normal score: `0.2806403814`
- CP963 LiteX-only x16 scores: `0.7767..0.7841`

So the current boundary is not simply "bladeRF hears nothing" or "LiteX TX is broken." The external bladeRF/RF path hears a strong signal, but the CP851 sequence correlation remains far below the clean LiteX-only recovery. Increasing amplitude helps but does not restore coherence linearly.

## Next step

Do not jump straight to higher TX amplitude. CP964 is already strong at bladeRF RX, so the next isolating rung should hold TX at x16 and vary bladeRF RX conditions:

- Run the same CP961 x16 external capture with lower bladeRF RX gain, for example `CAP_GAIN_DB=36`, to check for analog front-end compression or gain-state distortion.
- Keep all other CP920 settings unchanged and compare CP851 score, raw RMS, max i16, and spectrum peak SNR to CP964.

If lower RX gain improves correlation despite less digital amplitude, the failure mode is likely bladeRF RX/front-end linearity. If correlation falls with amplitude, the remaining issue is more likely external path SNR/coherence rather than simple clipping/compression.
