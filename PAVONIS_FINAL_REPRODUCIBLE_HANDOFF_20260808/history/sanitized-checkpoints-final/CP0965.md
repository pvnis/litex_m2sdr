## Interpretation

Lowering bladeRF RX gain from `42 dB` to `36 dB` reduced the digital capture level substantially:

- One-second raw RMS moved from CP964 `-8.44 dBFS` to CP965 `-14.58 dBFS`.
- Max i16 moved from `1807` to `879`.
- Spectrum RMS moved from `-10.44 dBFS` to `-16.57 dBFS`.

That did not improve CP851 recovery:

- CP964 gain 42: best `0.2806403814`, `normal`
- CP965 gain 36: best `0.2784527173`, `conjugate_capture`; best normal `0.2675932751`

So the simple theory that CP964 was primarily limited by bladeRF digital clipping or high-gain compression is not supported. CP965 still has a strong spectral peak at `24257.8 Hz` with `25.64 dB` peak SNR, but the sequence correlation remains partial and transform-ambiguous.

## Next step

The best current external 1SC result remains CP964 gain 42 x16. Since lowering gain did not help, the next useful isolation should target frequency/phase coherence rather than raw gain:

- Re-run x16 gain 42 with a narrower CP851 CFO/refinement window around the observed `0..5000 Hz` region and compare start stability, or
- Add a repeated capture at CP964 settings to test repeatability of the `normal`, `+[date-redacted] Hz`, `~0.28` result.

A repeatability check is the safer next rung before changing more variables.
