## Interpretation

The external path is not merely partially recoverable. It is cleanly recoverable over short windows:

- CP964 and CP966 both score about `0.95` with a `[date-redacted]`-sample template.
- Both select the expected `normal` transform at `[date-redacted] Hz`.
- CP965 gain36 also scores `0.952` at the same short-window setting, so the short-window signal is present even at lower gain.
- The x4 CP962 run also rises to `0.674` at `[date-redacted]`, well above the no-TX `0.142` short-window floor.

The failure appears when the correlation window gets longer:

- CP966 drops from `0.951` at `[date-redacted]` to `0.284` at `32768` and `0.201` at `65536`.
- CP964 shows the same pattern: `0.950` -> `0.281` -> `0.201`.
- The pattern repeats strongly enough that CP964 and CP966 should now be treated as a stable external baseline.

This changes the current diagnosis. The external bladeRF path is preserving local pieces of the one-subcarrier sequence very well, but it is not preserving phase/coherence over longer windows. The old CP851 `32768`-sample score understated the instantaneous recovery because it averaged across a window that is too long for the current external coherence behavior.

## Current boundary

The boundary is no longer:

- LiteX TX broken
- bladeRF RX silent
- bladeRF gain too high
- no useful correlation

The boundary is now:

- External CP961 x16/gain42 recovery is strong and repeatable over `[date-redacted]` samples.
- Recovery collapses progressively as the template grows to `4096`, `8192`, `16384`, `32768`, and `65536`.
- The likely failure class is phase/frequency drift, timing instability, or waveform coherence loss across several thousand samples.
