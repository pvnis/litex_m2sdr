## Interpretation

CP968 confirms that CP967's short-window recovery is not a single lucky alignment:

- CP964 x16/gain42 median chunk score is `0.847562`, with all 29 chunks above `0.747834`.
- CP966 repeat median chunk score is `0.843349`, with all 29 chunks above `0.758340`.
- The no-TX median is only `0.052129`, and its maximum is `0.141533`.
- CP962 x4 is also above no-TX, but lower than x16: median `0.574245`, max `0.674039`.

The short-window external path is therefore strong across the capture. The long-window collapse seen in CP967 is not caused by sparse signal presence.

The per-chunk CFO/start/phase is not stable enough for a simple long coherent integration:

- CP964 and CP966 both produce high local scores, but the selected CFO hops among `[date-redacted]`, `3000`, `4500`, and neighboring bins.
- The unwrapped phase spans are large (`~11..12 rad`) and should not be treated as a clean continuous phase trace because the best local start can jump between repeated sequence matches.
- The combination of high local scores and variable best CFO/start explains why `32768`-sample and `65536`-sample correlations collapse.

## Current boundary

The current external bladeRF path at CP961 x16/gain42 is:

- Strong and repeatable in spectrum.
- Cleanly recoverable over short windows.
- Stable enough to find high-scoring local matches throughout the capture.
- Not coherent enough for the old long-template CP851 pass criterion.

This points at a coherence/timing/CFO ambiguity problem rather than a TX silence, RX gain, or simple amplitude problem.
