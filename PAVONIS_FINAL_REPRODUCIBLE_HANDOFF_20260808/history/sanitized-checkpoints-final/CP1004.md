## Purpose

CP1004 continues the offline post-CP1002/CP1003 ladder with no additional RF
transmission.

CP1003 showed that CP1000/CP1001 active-`ATT22` external captures have strong
short-window hits but do not recover long coherence under simple piecewise
CFO/phase correction. CP1004 asks whether the remaining weakness is explained by
image/IQ behavior: the centered probe occupies bins `-1,0`, so a conjugated or
image-like observation should produce coherent mirrored energy at bins `+1,0`.

## Script

Script: `cp1004_two_subcarrier_image_mirror_analysis.py`

- the same comparison on non-DC bin `-1` versus `+1`, so the shared DC bin does
  not dominate the image metric

- phase residuals for occupied and mirrored channel estimates

## Interpretation

CP1004 separates two effects:

1. The external bladeRF captures have much more non-DC mirror-bin energy than
   the LiteX-only control.
2. That mirror-bin energy is not a clean coherent conjugated copy of the
   occupied reference.

Evidence for image leakage:

- CP1000/CP1001 long windows put non-DC mirror-bin power only about `9-11 dB`
  below the occupied non-DC bin.
- CP1000/CP1001 short windows put non-DC mirror-bin power only about `5.8-6.0 dB`
  below the occupied non-DC bin.
- CP1002 `phy` suppresses the non-DC mirror bin to the floor in the 32768 window
  and to about `-27.4 dB` in the 65536 window.

Evidence against a simple conjugation explanation:

- CP1000/CP1001 mirror image coherent scores are low: `0.016-0.090`.
- CP1000/CP1001 normal occupied scores remain weak, but still generally exceed
  the mirror coherent score in the long windows.
- CP1002 `phy` has strong normal occupied coherence around `0.965-0.976` while
  mirror coherence remains low.

So the CP967 normal/conjugate closeness is not explained by a clean IQ inversion
or simple mirrored signal. The external path appears to add significant
image-like leakage or adjacent-bin contamination, but with unstable phase or
insufficient coherent structure.

## Relation To CP1003

CP1003 ruled out simple per-chunk CFO/phase correction as the recovery path.
CP1004 adds that image/IQ asymmetry is materially worse in the external bladeRF
captures than in the LiteX-only source control.

Together, the current narrowed class is:

- bladeRF/external receive-path image leakage, I/Q imbalance, or adjacent-bin
  contamination is present;
- but it is not a single clean conjugated copy that can be corrected by choosing
  the mirrored transform;
- and the dominant decode blocker is still long-window phase/structure
  instability across the external observation path.

## Verdict

External-path non-DC mirror leakage: PRESENT.

Clean coherent conjugate/image explanation: FAIL.

LiteX-only `phy` control: PASS.

The first-failed boundary remains the external LiteX TX -> bladeRF RX observation
path, now with a concrete spectral-structure clue: strong short-window occupied
energy plus elevated non-DC mirror leakage, but no stable long coherent image.

## Next Step

The next useful rung should decide whether this image leakage is bladeRF receive
configuration dependent. A low-cost RF check would compare the same CP973 2SC x8
waveform under one bladeRF RX setting change at a time, starting with FIR/filter
state or RX bandwidth, while keeping active LiteX `ATT22` and the corrected
post-activate gain order fixed. If avoiding RF, the next offline step is to
measure whether the mirror leakage varies over time inside CP1000/CP1001 and
whether leakage bursts line up with low long-window coherence.
