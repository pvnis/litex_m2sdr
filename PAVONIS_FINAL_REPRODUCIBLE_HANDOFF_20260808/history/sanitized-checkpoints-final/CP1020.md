# Checkpoint 1020 - Negative 15 kHz Single Tone Explicit RX1/RX2 Pass

Date: [date-redacted]

## Purpose

CP1020 isolates the non-DC component of the CP1018/CP1019 stationary two-tone.

CP1015 proved a deterministic `500 kHz` tone is repeatable through the
installed LiteX TX to bladeRF RX1 path. CP1019 showed the stationary
two-tone at bins `-1,0` partially recovers long coherence compared with the
random-symbol CP1016 capture, but still does not match the LiteX-only baseline.

CP1020 asks whether a single close-in `-15 kHz` tone is already problematic, or
whether the issue only appears when the `-15 kHz` tone is combined with the DC
component or with symbol-to-symbol modulation.

## Interpretation

CP1020 rules against a simple "low-frequency non-DC tone is missing" theory:

- RX1 sees the `-15 kHz` single tone at the same level class as CP1015.
- RX2 remains much weaker, confirming RX1 is still the installed receive path.
- The tone has a stable FFT-window estimate on RX1, though shifted to about
  `-11.2 kHz`.

Combined with CP1019, this means the remaining partial-coherence issue likely
requires either the DC component, the interaction between DC and `-15 kHz`, or
symbol-to-symbol modulation. The next smallest isolation step is a DC-only
baseband tone/carrier test.

## Verdict

Single `-15 kHz` deterministic tone through explicit bladeRF RX1/RX2: PASS for
RX1, expected weak/WARN on RX2.

Near-DC non-DC single-tone path: PASS.

## Next Step

Run a DC-only deterministic baseband tone/carrier check with the same explicit
RX1/RX2 path. That will separate "DC component causes trouble" from "DC plus
`-15 kHz` interaction causes trouble."
