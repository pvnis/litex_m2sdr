## Interpretation

CP1038 falsifies the narrow theory that the remaining failure is caused solely by dropped samples or partial-write retry cadence. The TX helper wrote exactly the expected number of samples with `0` short writes, yet external RF correlation and tone stability got worse.

That does not mean chunking is irrelevant. CP1037 showed that true `3072` improved several short and medium windows, while CP1038 shows that a no-short-write `768` cadence is not automatically better. The impairment is more likely an interaction between TX write cadence, bladeRF/libbladeRF scheduling, RF/capture timing, and the external path, rather than a simple missing-samples bug.

The contemporaneous CP1035 LiteX-only baseline still passes, so this remains isolated away from the LiteX RX/TX/software baseline.

## Verdict

Keep the CP1034 partial-write fix. Do not adopt `768` as the next working setting. The next control should repeat the known-best explicit `1536` chunk with the now-fixed harness to check whether the external path has drifted/run-to-run degraded, before exploring another chunk split such as `384`, `1152`, or `2304`.
