CP1063 repeat capture could recover PBCH CRC OK from longer trimmed windows,
while the full one-second buffer still failed. CP1065 checks whether that
behavior is repeatable on the prior corrected ATT10/G42 CP1034 capture.

No RF hardware was used in this checkpoint.

## Comparison With CP1064

| Capture | Full one-second PBCH | Full one-second CSI PCI 1 | Trimmed cases | Trimmed PBCH OK | PBCH OK SSB offset |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| CP1062 | FAIL | `0/20` offsets | `162` | `19` | `0 Hz only` |
| CP1063 | FAIL | `1/20` offsets | `126` | `10` | `0 Hz only` |

Both corrected ATT10/G42 CP1034 captures now show the same local pattern:

- full one-second acquisition is not robust,
- sweeping the declared SSB frequency does not fix full-buffer PBCH,
- trimmed windows around the short-window success clusters recover PBCH,
- valid `ssb_file_test` PBCH recovery uses nominal SSB offset `0 Hz`.

## Interpretation

CP1065 strengthens the CP1064 conclusion. The external LiteX-to-bladeRF path can
carry PBCH-decodable samples in repeat captures, but the current full-buffer
acquisition path is sensitive to start/window contents and candidate selection.

The `+[date-redacted] Hz` reference-correlation peak should not be treated as the
`ssb_file_test` SSB frequency to use for PBCH. In both CP1064 and CP1065, PBCH
CRC OK appeared only at nominal SSB offset `0 Hz`.

## Verdict

CP1065: PASS for repeatability of trimmed-window PBCH recovery on CP1062; FAIL
for full one-second PBCH acquisition. Next debugging should focus on turning the
short/trimmed-window recovery into a deterministic acquisition strategy, not on
more TX power or RF retuning.
