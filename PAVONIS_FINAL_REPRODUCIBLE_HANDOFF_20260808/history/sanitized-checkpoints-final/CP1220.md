## Interpretation

CP1220 passes as a timestamped standalone replay comparison:

- CP1219 timestamped full-capture RX works for one-second RF captures.
- Both captures have complete sample count, no metadata gaps, and no explicit
  overrun/underrun counter hits.
- Both TX helpers are clean through the post-dedup release boundary.
- Both 50 ms and 75 ms lookahead captures contain decodable PBCH over a
  250 ms off-center sweep.

In this pair, 50 ms was stronger than 75 ms:

```text
50 ms: 26/847 PBCH OK, reference score 0.2921392294
75 ms: 19/847 PBCH OK, reference score 0.1558161233
```

This does not overturn CP1216/CP1217, where 75 ms passed strongly. It reinforces
the CP1213-CP1215 conclusion: short-window and one-shot labels are unstable
because capture-start phase and coherence move the PBCH-positive regions. A
250 ms or phase-aware analysis is required for paced-CS16 replay classification.

The bladeRF metadata timestamps are useful for capture continuity and chunk
integrity. They should not be over-interpreted as absolute LiteX-vs-bladeRF
hardware timing, because the first timestamp starts near stream enable on the
bladeRF side.

## Next Step

Do not repeat 50 ms or 75 ms merely to prove they can produce PBCH. The useful
next step is a small local post-analysis that combines:

- CP1220 bladeRF capture metadata and host epoch events,
- [host-redacted] replay schedule epoch events,
- reference-correlation best starts,
- 250 ms PBCH OK windows,
- CP1157/CP1145 metadata period mapping.

That table should say what CP1220 can and cannot tell us about scheduling
phase, then decide whether to instrument live gNB scheduling/capture placement
or build a minimal live-cadence replay rung.
