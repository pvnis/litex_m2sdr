## Conclusion

CP1223 is the strongest paired evidence so far for this boundary:

```text
Same live gNB window digital TX content: PASS
Same live gNB timed-DMA reconstruction: PASS
Same live gNB timestamped bladeRF RF capture: FAIL
```

The live gNB produced decodable PBCH in both pre-DMA and post-DMA digital
material, and the timed-DMA reconstruction contains many PBCH OK cases. The
paired bladeRF RF capture was continuous, unclipped, and cleanly captured, but
it had no PBCH OK windows and only two weak CSI PCI1 windows over 250 ms.

This closes the immediate theory that CP1222 failed because the live digital
material for that window was invalid or missing PBCH. The failure now sits
after valid timed digital DMA content, in the live RF egress/path/cadence
interaction or in how that material is physically emitted/observed by bladeRF.

## Next Step

Do not repeat CP1222 or CP1223 without a new variable. Useful next branches:

- Build a minimal live-cadence replay from CP1223's same-window DMA/pre-DMA
  material, preserving timing gaps and chunking but controlling the process.
- Add a tighter LiteX TX-to-bladeRF timing anchor that does not depend on
  cross-host clocks.
- Compare CP1223 DMA metadata timing and gap pattern against known-pass
  paced-CS16 replay timing to identify the live-only egress difference.

Keep Android and PRACH out of scope until independent downlink PBCH/NRSA is
stable again.
