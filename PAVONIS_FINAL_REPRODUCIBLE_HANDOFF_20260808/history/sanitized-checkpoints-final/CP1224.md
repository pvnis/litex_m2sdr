## Interpretation

CP1224 closes the idea that the live failure is explained by a gross pre-DMA
metadata-count mismatch or by a gross post-dedup HAS_TIME cadence mismatch.
CP1223 live pre-DMA has the same row count, sample total, `numElems`
histogram, flag histogram, input-offset histogram, timed-row count, and unique
timed timestamp delta distribution as the CP1220 known-pass controls.

The exact row placement is not identical: CP1223 differs from CP1220A/B in
136 flag positions and 2458 normalized relative-time row positions. That is a
real live-vs-paced metadata distinction, but it is not a basic count/cadence
failure. CP1223 actual DMA also shows timestamped rows at a ratio
(`0.068359`) very close to the CP1220 known-pass release-counter ratio
(`0.067453`).

The key limitation is that CP1220 did not capture actual DMA buffer metadata.
Therefore CP1224 cannot prove that CP1220 post-DMA gaps and CP1223 post-DMA
gaps are equivalent one-for-one. It can only compare CP1220 pre-DMA payload
metadata plus release counters against CP1223 pre-DMA metadata plus actual DMA
metadata.

## Status

CP1224 is a diagnostic pass. It narrows the next useful branch:

- do not repeat CP1220 50 ms or 75 ms paced-CS16 PBCH existence tests
- do not rerun a gross pre-DMA metadata-shape/count comparison
- do not rerun a gross HAS_TIME-cadence comparison
- next RF/debug rung should preserve or explain CP1223's exact live row
  placement and actual DMA timestamp/gap pattern, likely through a minimal
  live-cadence replay or a tighter LiteX-to-bladeRF egress/timing anchor
