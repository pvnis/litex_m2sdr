with the timestamped bladeRF harness. This tests whether the captured CP1223
actual-DMA samples, timed rows, and positive gap pattern are intrinsically
unable to produce RF PBCH, or whether they work when emitted by a controlled
helper.

This checkpoint used RF and SSH. It did not rebuild, use UE/Android, or run

## Interpretation

CP1227 proves that the captured CP1223 actual-DMA ch0 samples plus their
observed timed-row and positive-gap pattern can produce strong RF PBCH when
replayed through the LiteX TX to bladeRF RX path with a controlled CF32
metadata helper and the CP1226 period override.

This closes a narrower theory than CP1225:

- CP1225 showed CP1223 live pre-DMA material is replayable.
- CP1227 shows the captured CP1223 actual-DMA partial window and its gap
  pattern are also replayable.

The remaining live failure is therefore not explained by the first 512
actual-DMA rows being intrinsically bad, not explained by the observed
actual-DMA timed-row/gap pattern being unreplayable, and not explained by a
generic LiteX TX to bladeRF RX inability to decode that material.

The important limitation remains CP1226's partial-window caveat. CP1227 replayed
the first 512 actual-DMA rows and padded the loop period to CP1223's
`0.672611110 s` metadata span. It does not prove the missing `0.500167535 s`
tail of the live metadata span is good, nor does it prove the live gNB runtime
egress behaves like the controlled helper.

## Status

CP1227 is an RF pass and moves the boundary further down the live path. The
next useful branch is to capture or reproduce the missing live actual-DMA tail:
increase the live DMA dump depth enough to cover the full CP1223 metadata span,
or build a live-cadence helper that preserves the full-period schedule rather
than only the first 512 actual-DMA rows.
