## Comparison To CP1244

CP1244 at `TX_BACKOFF=5` with the same RX gain and frequency offset reached
`CAMPING`, then failed post-camping stability with `201` out-of-sync lines,
`360` RF overflows, and two RF gap/TX-error events.

CP1245 at `TX_BACKOFF=7` still reached an accepted expected-PCI1 PBCH hit
(`snr=+3.595 dB`) but did not synchronize SFN and never entered CAMPING.
Because it did not camp, it did not exercise the CP1244 post-camping stability
failure. The lower TX level reduces the evidence for RF-gap/out-of-sync
instability, but also gives up the CP1244 camping behavior in this run.

## Interpretation

TX_BACKOFF7 is not a stable downlink rung yet. It proves the initial NRSA
SNR/PBCH gate can still close at this level, but SFN/tracking remains
unreliable. The level bracket now looks like:

```text
TX_BACKOFF10: expected PCI1 PBCH candidates, SNR too low for acceptance
TX_BACKOFF7: accepted PCI1, SFN sync fail, no CAMPING
TX_BACKOFF5: accepted PCI1; with longer search, CAMPING then post-camping instability
```

The next useful rung should test the middle of this bracket or reduce RX stress
while preserving enough TX level to camp. A conservative next checkpoint is
`TX_BACKOFF=6`, RX gain `40`, `+3000 Hz`, max_trials `1000`,
post_select_ttis `1000`. If that camps but has fewer overflows/out-of-sync
than CP1244, the issue is level/overdrive sensitive. If it behaves like CP1245,
then TX_BACKOFF5 remains the only observed camping live level and RX gain or
post-select behavior should be isolated there.
