## Comparison To CP1244 And CP1245

CP1244 at `TX_BACKOFF=5`, RX gain `40`, and `+3000 Hz` reached `CAMPING`,
then failed post-camping stability with `201` out-of-sync lines, `360` RF
overflows, and two RF gap/TX-error events.

CP1245 at `TX_BACKOFF=7` accepted expected PCI1 at call/trial `521` with
`snr=+3.595 dB`, then failed SFN synchronization after `100` SFN trials and
never entered `CAMPING`; it had `115` RF overflow lines, no out-of-sync, and
no RF-gap/TX-failed lines.

CP1246 at `TX_BACKOFF=6` accepted expected PCI1 earlier, at call/trial `159`,
with `snr=-2.194 dB`, then also failed SFN synchronization after `100` SFN
trials and never entered `CAMPING`. It had fewer RF overflows (`30`) and no
out-of-sync or RF-gap/TX-failed lines.

## Interpretation

TX_BACKOFF6 is not a stable downlink rung yet. It does not reproduce CP1244's
camping behavior, even though it closes the initial NRSA SNR/PBCH gate. The
level bracket now looks like:

```text
TX_BACKOFF10: expected PCI1 PBCH candidates, SNR too low for acceptance
TX_BACKOFF7: accepted PCI1, SFN sync fail, no CAMPING, 115 RF overflows
TX_BACKOFF6: accepted PCI1, SFN sync fail, no CAMPING, 30 RF overflows
TX_BACKOFF5: accepted PCI1; with longer search, CAMPING then post-camping instability
```

Lowering TX level from 5 to 6/7 reduces the visible overflow/out-of-sync
severity, but in these two runs it also gives up the only observed live NRSA
camping behavior. The next useful rung should keep `TX_BACKOFF=5` and reduce
RX stress instead, for example RX gain `36` or `38` with the same `+3000 Hz`,
`max_trials=1000`, and `post_select_ttis=1000`. That isolates whether CP1244's
post-camping instability is bladeRF RX overdrive/overflow while preserving the
TX level that has already reached CAMPING once.
