## Comparison To CP1244 And CP1246

CP1244 at `TX_BACKOFF=5`, RX gain `40`, and `+3000 Hz` reached CAMPING, then
failed post-camping stability with `201` out-of-sync lines, `360` RF overflows,
and two RF gap/TX-error events.

CP1246 at `TX_BACKOFF=6`, RX gain `40`, and `+3000 Hz` accepted PCI1 but
never camped. It failed all 100 SFN-sync trials and had `30` RF overflows, no
out-of-sync lines, and no RF-gap/TX-failed lines.

CP1247 proves RX gain `36` does not prevent camping at `TX_BACKOFF=5`: it
accepted PCI1 at trial `270`, synchronized SFN on trial `2`, and entered
CAMPING. It did not fix the post-camping failure class. It reduced RF overflow
count versus CP1244 (`124` vs `360`) and reduced RF-gap/TX-failed pairs
(`1` vs `2`), but out-of-sync lines increased (`447` vs `201`).

## Interpretation

Lowering bladeRF RX gain from `40` to `36` does not eliminate the live
post-camping instability at the only camping TX level. The result weakens a
simple "RX gain 40 overdrive alone causes the failure" theory, but it does not
close the broader RX-stress branch because overflow and RF-gap symptoms were
reduced while CAMPING was preserved.

The next useful rung is a lower RX-gain test at the same `TX_BACKOFF=5`, such
as RX gain `32`, because CP1247 still has enough acquisition margin
(`snr=-3.466 dB`, above the `-10 dB` gate) and still shows overflow/gap
symptoms. If lower RX gain preserves camping and reduces out-of-sync/gap
behavior, the branch remains RX-stress sensitive. If it still fails with few
or no overflows, the next branch should instrument post-camping timing/tracking
rather than continue simple gain changes.
