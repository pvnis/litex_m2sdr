## Conclusion

CP1262 changes the frontier.

Widening the absolute delay gate to `60 us` recovered SFN sync, CAMPING, and
two TRACK periods. However, normal scheduled TRACK PBCH decode failed on all
40 opportunities. The same-buffer blind sidecheck found CRC-valid SSBs on
2/40 opportunities, and the rolling-tail sidecheck found exactly the same
2/40 hits.

Those two hits did not cross the previous/current 1 ms boundary:
`search_crosses_current_boundary=0` both times. Therefore the CP1260
rolling-tail boundary theory is not the explanation for these hits. The
stronger new suspect is the scheduled TRACK decode placement/state: it was
using `selected_sf_offset=825`, while blind search found decodable SSBs at
current-buffer offsets `296` and `2736`.

This also refines CP1259. CP1259 showed same-buffer sidecheck `0/20` under the
30 us gate; CP1262 shows same-buffer blind search can occasionally decode
under the 60 us-gated TRACK state. The immediate mismatch is scheduled TRACK
offset/frequency/state versus blind SSB search, not simply "the current 1 ms
buffer is never decodable" and not solely a rolling-buffer boundary issue.

Recommended next rung: instrument or experimentally use the blind sidecheck
hit as an opt-in TRACK fallback/resync candidate, with explicit logging of the
offset delta from scheduled `selected_sf_offset` before changing default
behavior.
