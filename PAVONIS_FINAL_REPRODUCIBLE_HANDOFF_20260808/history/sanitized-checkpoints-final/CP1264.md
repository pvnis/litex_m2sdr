## Rolling-Only Boundary Hit

CP1264 also exposed a new useful discriminator before the same-buffer fallback.
At opportunity 3, same-buffer blind search failed, but rolling search found a
CRC-valid SSB crossing the previous/current 1 ms boundary:

```text
track_sidecheck sidecheck=3 search_crc=0
track_rolling_sidecheck rolling_sidecheck=3 search_crc=1 search_N_id=1 search_t_offset=1360 search_current_offset=-284 search_crosses_current_boundary=1 search_snr=-18.133 search_cfo=-368.135 search_delay_us=-5.089882
track_opportunity opportunity=3 ... pbch_crc=0 miss_after=3 ... track_rolling_sidecheck_hits=1
```

This does not contradict CP1262, where the two rolling hits were not
boundary-crossing. It refines the picture: same-buffer hits can rescue some
misses, but rolling-only boundary hits also happen and are currently ignored
by the CP1263 fallback.

## Conclusion

CP1264 proves the same-buffer blind fallback behavior works and can extend the
TRACK period by resetting the miss streak when blind search finds the SSB at a
different offset than scheduled TRACK. In this run, the fallback delayed the
first 20-miss drop from the original cadence to opportunity 30.

It is not sufficient by itself. Scheduled TRACK PBCH still failed on all
50 opportunities (`pbch_crc=1` count `0`), and the UE ultimately dropped
twice. The next higher-value rung is an opt-in rolling blind fallback/resync
path, because CP1264 captured a rolling-only, boundary-crossing CRC-valid SSB
at opportunity 3 that same-buffer fallback could not use.
