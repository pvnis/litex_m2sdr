## Conclusion

CP1266 proves the rolling fallback path works and can consume a
boundary-crossing rolling blind-search hit. It also confirms fallback resets
can extend TRACK: the first drop moved to opportunity 37, and the UE was still
in TRACK at process exit after a second reacquisition.

It is still not sufficient. Scheduled TRACK PBCH remained `0/59`, and all
three fallback hits came from blind search at offsets far from the scheduled
`selected_sf_offset=825` (`-734`, `2740`, and `2690` in current-buffer
coordinates). Feeding blind hits through `ue_sync_nr_update_ssb()` updates
CFO/delay and resets misses, but it does not make the scheduled TRACK path
land on the SSB.

Next rung should stop adding fallback resets and instead test the timebase
effect of blind fallback. The most direct opt-in branch is a feedback-only
fallback mode that applies CFO/delay from the blind hit but does not let the
blind PBCH MIB reset `q->sfn`, `q->sf_idx`, or `ssb_idx`; CP1266 shows those
MIB-derived updates can jump the TRACK timeline while scheduled TRACK remains
misplaced.
