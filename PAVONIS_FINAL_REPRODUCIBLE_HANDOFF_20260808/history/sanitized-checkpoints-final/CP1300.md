Date: [date-redacted] Europe/Zurich

## Goal

CP1290-CP1299 showed that simple TX backoff, frequency-offset, and srsUE RX
gain brackets do not raise the scheduled TRACK raw downlink gate. CP1300 keeps
the same no-anchor raw metric and tests one mid-high srsUE bladeRF RX gain
point, `44 dB`, while enabling the existing RF blade state debug hook to prove
whether the requested gain is actually applied by the srsUE bladeRF plugin.

The gate remains scheduled `event=track_opportunity` mean `meas_snr` and the
percentage of opportunities at or above `-10 dB`.

## Conclusion

CP1300 is a negative mid-high-RX-gain raw-DL bracket, but it adds one useful
fact: the srsUE bladeRF plugin does apply the requested RX gain in the active
stream state.

At `TX_BACKOFF=5`, srsUE RX gain `44`, and `rf.freq_offset=+3000 Hz`, live
NRSA reaches CAMPING/TRACK but scheduled TRACK produces `87` opportunities,
`0` PBCH hits, mean `meas_snr=-21.645 dB`, and `0/87` opportunities at or
above `-10 dB`.

The active gate remains unchanged: do not continue `ue_sync_nr.c` sync/anchor
logic work until the raw live TRACK-opportunity SNR and usable fraction are
raised. RX gain `44` is closed as a simple raw-budget improvement at this RF
point, and future raw-budget work can trust that `rf.rx_gain` is not simply
being ignored by the srsUE bladeRF path.
