## Goal

## Regression/Caveat

The same-run digital TX guard regressed after enabling the 2M-sample write
dump:

- `PRE_DMA_PBCH_OK=1`
- `POST_DMA_PBCH_OK=0`
- `DIGITAL_TX_PBCH_OK=0`

CP1400 without the write dump had same-run post-DMA/digital PBCH OK. CP1405
therefore should not be treated as a clean live digital-TX-OK sidecar run.
The write-boundary evidence is still valid, but the 16 MB synchronous dump from
the live TX path may perturb timing/downstream DMA capture, or it may expose a
downstream egress issue below the C++ `writeStream()` call.

## Interpretation

CP1405 strongly weakens the theory that the live gNB weak append is caused by
the OCUDU C++ CI16-to-CF32 conversion/chunking path itself. The samples handed
to Soapy `writeStream()` are high-level and near-identical to the pre-DMA
reference, while the gNB-owned RX append remains about 25 dB below standalone
LiteX replay.

The remaining suspect area is below or after the C++ `writeStream()` boundary:
Soapy/M2SDR plugin egress, DMA/timed-TX serialization, AD9361 TX presentation,
or a real live TX/RX coupling/link-budget condition. Because CP1405 also
regressed post-DMA digital decode, the next immediate diagnostic should use a
much smaller write-dump cap or a metadata-only write trace to check whether the
large synchronous dump perturbs the downstream path before relying on it for
more RF comparisons.
