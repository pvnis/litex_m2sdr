## Interpretation

CP1435 is the requested A/B against CP1434's leading ambiguity:

- Historical CP1382 used this capture class at the same `7.68 Msps` /
  `6.144 MHz` SSB-centered receive geometry and decoded RF: one-shot PBCH CRC
  `OK`, `36/847` aligned PBCH-OK windows, raw max `343`, and RMS rel
  `0.024506723`.
- CP1434 used a CP1433-style `CF32` plus self-RX gNB window and failed
  independent bladeRF PBCH (`0/847`) while same-window M2SDR self-RX and TX
  guard were positive.
- CP1435 removes that CP1433/CP1434 window difference by rerunning the older
  CP1382 class, but it still fails independent bladeRF PBCH (`0/847`) even
  though the capture stream is continuous/unclipped and corrected TX guard is
  positive.

This weakens the theory that the CP1434 failure was caused specifically by
the M2SDR self-RX instrument or live `CF32` TX window. It strengthens the
theory that the external bladeRF/OTA receive path has degraded since the
historical CP1382 pass, or that there is a transient/environmental receive-path
state not captured by the gNB digital TX guard.

CP1435 is also not evidence for an antenna-overheating failure: the observed
bladeRF device temperature and power were normal for the recent runs, and the
M2SDR self-RX path decoded in CP1433/CP1434. The problem remains localized to
the external bladeRF/OTA receive path or its immediate RF/control state unless
future A/B tests show otherwise.

Next useful work should stay off scheduler logic and re-establish a physical
receive baseline: either recheck another historical independent bladeRF pass
geometry, such as the CP1362/CP1343 `5.76 Msps` SSB-centered point or the
CP1313 carrier-centered capture point, or run a small bladeRF RX-path sanity
checkpoint that records RX mux/loopback/filter/gain/RSSI/noise state before
more live scheduled TRACK runs.
