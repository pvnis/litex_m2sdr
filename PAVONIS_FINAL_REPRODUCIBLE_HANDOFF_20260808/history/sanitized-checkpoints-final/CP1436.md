## Interpretation

Historical CP1362 used this capture class at the same `5.76 Msps` /
`4.608 MHz` SSB-centered receive geometry and decoded RF: one-shot PBCH CRC
`OK`, CSI SNR `+10.0 dB`, `35/847` aligned PBCH-OK windows, raw max `335`,
and RMS rel `0.024852554`.

CP1436 repeats that class and now fails independent bladeRF PBCH completely:
one-shot CRC `KO`, aligned sweep `0/847`, raw max `137`, and RMS rel
`0.016877590`. The capture stream itself is clean, bladeRF temperatures and
power are normal, post-check `[host-redacted]` reachability passes, and corrected
gNB TX still contains decodable PBCH before and after DMA.

Together with CP1435, this means two historical independent bladeRF pass
geometries now fail while the corrected gNB digital TX guard remains positive:

- CP1382-class `7.68 Msps` / `6.144 MHz`: historical pass `36/847`, CP1435
  recheck `0/847`.
- CP1362-class `5.76 Msps` / `4.608 MHz`: historical pass `35/847`, CP1436
  recheck `0/847`.

That strongly weakens CP1434-specific explanations such as the M2SDR self-RX
instrument or live `CF32` TX window. The leading issue is now a broad external
bladeRF/OTA receive-path degradation or receive-path control state, not
scheduler/anchor logic and not gNB digital TX content.

Next useful work should be a bladeRF receive-path sanity checkpoint, not
another scheduled TRACK rung: record or reset bladeRF RX mux/loopback/filter/
gain state, compare no-TX noise/RSSI to TX-on state, and only then rerun an
independent raw-DL capture. If that remains low, the physical antenna/cable/
receive-chain path is the likely blocker.
