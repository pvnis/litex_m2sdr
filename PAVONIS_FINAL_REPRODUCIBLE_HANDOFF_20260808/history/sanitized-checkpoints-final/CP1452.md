bladeRF-side external capture at the same commanded TX window looked
high-level but tone-unstable. CP1452 tests whether that bladeRF-side level and

## Interpretation

CP1452 reinterprets the bladeRF half of CP1451. The clean LiteX/M2SDR self-RX
side of CP1451 still proves that the M2SDR TX path generated a stable tone in
that same commanded window. However, the bladeRF-side CP1451 capture did not
show evidence of receiving that tone above the bladeRF receive-path floor at
the tested settings.

At gain48, BW3.84 MHz, filter bypass, and SSB-centered RX, the bladeRF path
already produces a `-19.3 dBFS` no-TX floor/spur-like capture with an apparent
FFT peak near the CP1451 expected `+500 kHz` bin. Therefore the CP1451
external "high-level bad tone" should be treated as a no-TX-like bladeRF
receive-path artifact plus little or no observable incremental LiteX TX
signal, not as a real high-level but frequency-unstable LiteX tone.

This strengthens the external receive-path/coupling/routing/front-end branch:

```text
M2SDR TX/self-RX at CP1451: clean and stable
bladeRF RX at CP1451 geometry with TX on: no better than no-TX
bladeRF RX route during capture: RX1 <= RF3(B), expected installed path
bladeRF internal RFIC BIST in CP1448: clean
```

The likely fault surface is now narrower than after CP1451: external coupling
from LiteX TX to bladeRF RF3(B), the physical installed receive path into
bladeRF RX1/RF3(B), or a bladeRF external-RX front-end/path state that presents
a high local floor and masks the transmitted signal. CP1452 does not raise the
raw scheduled TRACK gate and is not a PRACH/attach ladder unlock.

## Do Not Repeat

Do not use CP1451's bladeRF raw level alone as evidence that the external tone
was received. It is now matched by a no-TX capture.

Do not rerun CP1451 unchanged. If tone work continues, use an explicit on/off
differential design, preferably at lower gain or with enough geometry changes
to reduce the no-TX floor before declaring external tone recovery.

## Next

The next useful RF-active checkpoint should be a true on/off differential, not
another standalone TX-on tone capture. Good candidates:

```text
1. Low-gain on/off tone pair at the same geometry or narrower BW, to find any
   incremental LiteX signal above the bladeRF floor.
2. Physical path/coupling inspection or controlled cable/coupling/attenuator
   swap, if available.
3. If returning to live NR, only do so after a receive-path change raises the
   raw TRACK metric or after the bladeRF path shows a real TX-on delta.
```
