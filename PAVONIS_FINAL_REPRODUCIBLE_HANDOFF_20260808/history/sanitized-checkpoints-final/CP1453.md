# Checkpoint 1453 - BW2.0 Bypass Gain48 TX-On/TX-Off Differential

Date: [date-redacted] Europe/Zurich

## Purpose

CP1452 showed that CP1451's bladeRF-side `BW3.84 MHz`/bypass/gain48
"high-level tone" was matched by a no-TX bladeRF capture. CP1453 repeats that
logic at the narrower receive setting where CP1445 previously looked best:
`BW2.0 MHz`, bypass, gain48, SSB-centered bladeRF RX, expected `+500 kHz`
capture tone.

The question is whether the CP1445 high-SNR narrow-BW tone was real TX energy
or another bladeRF no-TX floor/spur class.

The TX-on bladeRF RMS is only about `0.05 dB` above no-TX:

```text
20*log10(0.081203 / 0.080734) ~= +0.05 dB
```

That is not a usable external tone delta. The no-TX bladeRF capture already
contains the same pseudo-tone class near the expected `+500 kHz` region and the
same wide 10 ms frequency-window spread.

## Interpretation

CP1453 extends CP1452 from `BW3.84 MHz` to the CP1445 `BW2.0 MHz` setting.
Even at the narrower BW2.0/bypass/gain48 point, bladeRF TX-on is essentially
the same as no-TX, while the M2SDR self-RX confirms the transmitter produced a
clean stable tone in-window.

Therefore CP1445's high-SNR narrow-BW tone result should not be treated as
proof of a recovered external LiteX-to-bladeRF tone path. It is consistent
with a bladeRF receive-path floor/spur/pseudo-tone class that exists without
the M2SDR transmitter. The active route is still the installed RX1/RF3(B)
path, so this points back to external coupling/path/front-end state rather than
M2SDR TX generation.

CP1453 is not a raw scheduled TRACK metric datapoint and does not unlock the
ladder. The raw gate remains the mean TRACK-opportunity SNR and `% >= -10 dB`.

## Do Not Repeat

Do not rerun the CP1445/CP1453 BW2.0/bypass/gain48 tone setup unchanged and
interpret the bladeRF pseudo-tone as received LiteX TX. Any further tone work
needs a real TX-on/TX-off delta, a lower-floor receive state, a physical path
change, or an independent receiver.

## Next

Further remote-only debugging is now mostly proving absence through the same
installed bladeRF RX path. The next useful progress likely requires one of:

```text
1. Physical path/coupling inspection or controlled cable/coupling/attenuator
   swap into bladeRF RX1/RF3(B).
2. Independent receiver/spectrum observation at the bladeRF input point.
3. A bladeRF receive-state change that demonstrably reduces the no-TX
   floor/spur and produces a measurable TX-on delta before returning to raw
   scheduled TRACK.
```
