# Checkpoint 1454 - BW2.0 Bypass Gain32 TX-On/TX-Off Differential

Date: [date-redacted] Europe/Zurich

## Purpose

CP1453 closed the CP1445 `BW2.0 MHz`/bypass/gain48 tone point as
no-TX-like: bladeRF TX-on and no-TX differed by only about `+0.05 dB`, while
M2SDR self-RX was clean. CP1454 repeats the same explicit on/off differential
at lower bladeRF gain32.

This tests a narrower question: if the gain48 no-TX spur/floor is masking a
real weak signal, does lowering bladeRF gain to 32 create a lower-floor receive
state where a TX-on delta appears?

The TX-on bladeRF RMS is slightly lower than no-TX, effectively zero delta:

```text
20*log10(0.013351 / 0.013354) ~= -0.002 dB
```

Lowering gain from 48 to 32 did reduce the bladeRF no-TX floor by about
`15.6 dB` compared with CP1453A:

```text
20*log10(0.013354 / 0.080734) ~= -15.6 dB
```

But the TX-on leg followed it exactly. There is still no detectable external
tone delta at the bladeRF input/path.

## Interpretation

CP1454 is stronger than CP1453 in one important way: it proves a lower-floor
bladeRF receive state is possible at gain32, but even in that lower-floor state
the M2SDR TX-on capture is indistinguishable from no-TX. The M2SDR self-RX
again proves that the transmitter generated a stable tone in-window.

This closes the simple theory that gain48's high no-TX floor was merely
masking a received LiteX tone and that lowering gain would reveal it. The
remaining leading explanation is external coupling/path/front-end state between
M2SDR TX and bladeRF RX1/RF3(B), or a bladeRF external-RX path problem that is
not corrected by gain32/gain48, BW2.0/BW3.84, bypass/normal filter, host/FPGA
[interface-redacted] mode, or RFIC BIST.

CP1454 is not a raw scheduled TRACK metric datapoint and does not unlock the
ladder. The raw gate remains mean TRACK-opportunity SNR and `% >= -10 dB`, but
returning to raw TRACK before a physical/independent receive-path change is
unlikely to add information.

## Do Not Repeat

Do not rerun BW2.0/bypass gain32 or gain48 tone on/off differentials unchanged.
Both show no meaningful TX-on bladeRF delta while M2SDR self-RX is clean.

Do not treat CP1445's narrow-BW tone PASS or CP1451's high-level bladeRF raw
as evidence that LiteX TX reaches bladeRF RX; both are now contradicted by
no-TX or on/off controls.
