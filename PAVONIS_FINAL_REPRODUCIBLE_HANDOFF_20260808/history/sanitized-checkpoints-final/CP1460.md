# Checkpoint 1460: BW6.144MHz Normal-Filter Gain48 TX-Off/TX-On Differential

## Purpose

`6.144 MHz` RX bandwidth, normal RX FIR filter, and gain48, but it was a
TX-on-only gain sweep. CP1460 closes that missing no-TX control by repeating
the same receive geometry with TX off, then TX on with same-window M2SDR

## Interpretation

CP1460 closes CP1442's missing no-TX control. At the default
`6.144 MHz` bandwidth, normal filter, and gain48, the bladeRF capture is
effectively unchanged when the M2SDR transmitter is turned on
(`+0.071 dB` RMS), while the M2SDR self-RX instrument sees a clean `80.35 dB`
tone in the same window.

Together with CP1452 through CP1459, this closes the current safe external-tone
software-only receive setting bracket: BW6.144 normal, BW3.84 normal, BW3.84
bypass, BW2.0 bypass, BW2.0 normal, gain32 versus gain48, the `+100 kHz` tone
placement, retune-reset, and explicit RX2 do not reveal a received bladeRF
tone when controlled against no-TX. The leading branch remains external
bladeRF/OTA receive/coupling/routing/front-end state or physical path, not
live M2SDR TX, the M2SDR self-RX instrument, simple bladeRF bandwidth/filter
mode, gain48 floor masking alone, or scheduler/anchor logic.

CP1460 does not raise the raw scheduled TRACK gate and does not justify new
scheduler/anchor edits. The next meaningful ladder step needs a physical or
independent receive-path change, or explicit approval for a hardware-affecting
bladeRF state branch such as calibration/correction or bias-tee experiments.
