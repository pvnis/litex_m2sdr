## Goal

CP1484 CSI-only/PBCH-negative high-correlation window to a small top-three set.

recoverable PBCH when timing/CFO/half-frame are forced.

## Interpretation

CP1491 confirms CP1490's forced PBCH recovery is real, but not universal across
the high PBCH-region-correlation CP1484 CSI-only class. High PBCH-symbol
correlation is therefore not sufficient by itself to predict a recoverable
PBCH payload under the bounded forced timing/CFO/half-frame sweep.

The frontier narrows further: one CP1484 degraded-helper window contains
PBCH-decodable content that the blind path misses, while nearby/high-correlation
windows still fail even under forced tracking. The next useful no-RF diagnostic
is to compare successful versus failed forced-track rows at PBCH DMRS,
selection, and handoff level, rather than treating raw power/path as the
primary branch.

Given the user's latest instruction, the next RF checkpoint should also repeat
the continuous-helper versus live-gNB back-to-back comparison on the same
hardware as a fresh cadence/coherence sample, without starting a hardware
branch.

## Verdict

CP1491 strengthens the decoder-facing handoff theory but does not clear the
live cadence/coherence gate. Do not rerun only the same forced top-three sweep
unchanged. If continuing offline, inspect blind-search PBCH selection versus
forced-track success. If continuing RF per current user direction, rerun the
continuous helper and live gNB back-to-back on the same hardware and compare
raw amplitude, PBCH/CSI counts, same-window correlation, and host-visible TX
guards.
