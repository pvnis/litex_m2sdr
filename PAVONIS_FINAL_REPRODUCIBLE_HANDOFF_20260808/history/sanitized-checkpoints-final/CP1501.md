## Goal

Follow CP1500's next offline step: compare SSB-region coherence on the exact
six CP1500 vectors, without running RF or changing hardware. The intent was to
see whether PSS/SSS or PBCH-region correlation explains why CP1499 live
`0.159 s` is forced-decodable while live `0.069 s` remains unrecovered.

No PRACH/Android/NAS step, hardware-affecting action, or `ue_sync_nr.c`
sync/anchor edit was performed.

The script reads CP1500's `candidates.csv` and `forced_track_sweep.csv`,
selects each candidate's best forced-track row, demodulates the local SSB grid
at that `t_offset`/CFO, and compares five SSB regions to the best matching
helper PBCH-OK control:

- `sym0_pss127`
- `sym1_pbch240`
- `sym2_sss127`
- `sym2_pbch_outer113`
- `sym3_pbch240`

Important limitation: this is a cross-frame/control-relative comparison, not a
same-payload phase-calibrated reference like CP1489. PSS/SSS correlations are
more meaningful than PBCH-bearing regions; PBCH-region correlations are
payload-sensitive.

## Interpretation

This metric is not a reliable standalone discriminator. The two helper PBCH-OK
controls correlate weakly with each other (`PSS/SSS 0.1596/0.1164`) despite
both being known-decodable, so cross-control SSB-region correlation is not
stable enough to explain live recovery by itself.

The useful negative result is that PSS/SSS control-correlation does not predict
forced PBCH recovery here:

- helper `0.190 s` has high PSS/SSS control-correlation and is forced-decodable
- live `0.159 s` has low PSS/SSS control-correlation and is still
  forced-decodable
- live `0.069 s` has moderate PSS/SSS control-correlation and remains
  unrecovered

Therefore the next useful offline step is not more broad region correlation.
It should instrument the local blind-search decision path on the selected
vectors: public search candidate timing, PBCH DMRS threshold/metric, and why
live `0.159 s` forced-decodes but public search does not, while live `0.069 s`
does not forced-decode.
