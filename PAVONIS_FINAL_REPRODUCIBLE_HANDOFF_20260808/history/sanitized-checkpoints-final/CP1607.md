## Conclusion

CP1607 is a clean positive checkpoint.

With normal live gNB TX stream I/O restored, the controlled CP19 PRACH remains
visible in the gNB-owned RX-CF32 append, visible in PRACH predemod windows, and
accepted by the PRACH detector as preamble 0.

This rules out a broad explanation that active live TX stream/full-duplex state
alone prevents PRACH visibility or detector acceptance. The remaining gap is
between continuously repeated controlled CP19 and the real sparse UE PRACH
attempts from CP1576/CP1569.

## Next

The next rung should keep normal live TX stream I/O and the CP1607 analyzer,
but make the controlled CP19 transmission sparse and UE-like: a small number of
bursts aligned to PRACH occasions rather than a continuous repeated waveform.
That separates "detector accepts a high-duty controlled PRACH sequence" from
"detector accepts a real-duration PRACH burst at the intended occasion."
