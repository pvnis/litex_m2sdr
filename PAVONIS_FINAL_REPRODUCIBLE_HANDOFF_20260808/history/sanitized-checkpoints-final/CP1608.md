## Goal

Keep CP1607's normal live gNB TX stream I/O and PRACH trace/predemod analyzer,
but change the [host-redacted] transmitter from continuously repeated CP19 to sparse
UE-like controlled CP19 bursts.

This tests whether CP1607's positive detector result depends on high-duty
continuous/repeated controlled PRACH, versus a real-duration sparse burst class
closer to the CP1569/CP1576 UE failures.

## Conclusion

CP1608 is a clean mechanical checkpoint and a useful negative.

The sparse controlled CP19 file was transmitted under the same normal live
TX-stream state that passed in CP1607. The gNB-owned RX-CF32 append still sees
limited CP19 energy in the full 4 s file, with two strong append windows and a
best normalized score around `0.501`. However, those sparse bursts do not land
in the live PRACH predemod windows with useful score, and the detector accepts
zero rows.

This means the CP1607 pass does not transfer automatically from continuously
repeated controlled CP19 to sparse UE-like bursts. The failure is not explained
by RF path loss or normal live TX stream state alone; it is tied to sparse
burst duty/phase/occasion alignment relative to the gNB predemod/detector
windows.

## Next

Keep the CP1607/CP1608 normal live TX-stream analyzer, but phase-sweep sparse
CP19 timing against the PRACH occasion grid. A good next rung is a sparse
multi-phase helper or small phase sweep across one PRACH period, then analyze
which phase intersects predemod rows before trying another real UE PRACH run.
