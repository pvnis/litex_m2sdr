## Verdict

CP1682 is the valid two-point native-continuous `10 ms` marker result. It does
not clear the single-anchor path. The early marker lands at about `+0.740 ms`,
while the `+60 s` marker lands at about `+9.175 ms`, an early/late spread of
about `8.435 ms`.

This supports the user's concern: an early-only marker near the start would
have falsely suggested a low/stable offset, while the late marker shows a
millisecond-scale run-time offset growth/jump in the same stream. For any
future CP1624-style delay value or real-UE timing interpretation on native
single-anchor TX, do not use the early marker alone.

Next changed work should use this result to choose between:

- restoring all-timed or another stable-timing TX presentation before SIB1/PRACH
  ladder movement, or
- treating native-continuous single-anchor timing as epoch-dependent and
  measuring the relevant epoch before applying any delay/window value.
