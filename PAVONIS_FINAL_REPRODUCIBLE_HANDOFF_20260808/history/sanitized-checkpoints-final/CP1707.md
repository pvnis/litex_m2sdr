## SI result

CP1694's passing SI row is slot `9601`, `n_dl=1`,
`max_corr=0.797950`, `max_epre=22.977`. The mapped TX summary still has a
large legacy `samples_req - samples_ret` deficit of `29311450` samples
(`2544.397 ms`) and `28266` timeouts, but that run had no timeout-carry path
to preserve stale timed chunks.

CP1698 is not missing SI energy. Across `800` SI summaries it has
`n_dl_positive=0`, max/mean/median `max_corr`
`0.409127/0.248491/0.249704`, `0` rows at or above `0.5`, and max/mean/min
`max_epre` `23.191/22.400/22.120`.

The specific CP1694-vs-CP1698 difference is TX chunk timing/backlog around SI:

- CP1698 top 12 SI-correlation rows carry `8.202..18.379 ms` of
  `timeout_carry_pending_samples` at the mapped TX summary, mean `13.903 ms`.
- CP1698 rows covering CP1694's passing slot `9601` carry
  `8.024..18.379 ms` pending, mean `13.905 ms`.
- Those CP1698 rows still show LiteX worker/release late counters `0/0`.
- The per-period deltas around representative failed SI windows keep queuing
  about `1200` carry chunks while draining `~1087..1264` chunks and recording
  `~87..91` carry drain timeouts.

Conclusion: CP1698 proves stop-time `stream_deficit=0` is not sufficient.
The live stream can be sample-count complete at stop while SI slots are still
transmitted behind a multi-ms stale timed-data queue. That maps directly to
the observed correlation collapse from CP1694's `0.80` class to CP1698's
`0.41` class.
