## Interpretation

The pair classification still matches CP1719:

- RX1 remains the cabled/attenuated path in software evidence. It rises
  `+14.04 dB` above the CP1717 no-TX floor and CP847 passes at the expected
  tone frequency class.
- RX2 remains the antenna/weak-leakage path. Its RMS is only `+0.20 dB` above
  no-TX, matching CP1719's weak-path amplitude class.

The only regression versus CP1719 is RX2 tone quality: CP1719 had a weak but
CP847-positive expected-frequency tone on RX2; CP1722 is still only
floor-class in amplitude and CP847 now fails because the dominant peak is
about `18.5 kHz` away from the expected `+500 kHz` tone. CP892 still sees weak
energy near the expected bin in window estimates, but it is not strong enough
to treat RX2 as a usable cabled path.

## Verdict

CP1722 passes as a hardware-path recheck for RX1/RX2 classification. Use RX1
as the current cabled/attenuated receive path. Treat RX2 as the antenna or
weak-leakage path and do not rely on it for cabled ladder datapoints unless a
future physical label or swapped-cable test proves otherwise.
