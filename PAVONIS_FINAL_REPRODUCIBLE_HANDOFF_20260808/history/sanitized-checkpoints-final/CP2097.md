## CP2057 RAR Classification

The gNB did transmit RAR:

```text
[405.8] Slot decisions ... RAR: ra-rnti=0x39 rb=[0..3) tbs=9
[629.8] Slot decisions ... RAR: ra-rnti=0x39 rb=[0..3) tbs=9
```

The UE did not decode any RA-PDCCH or RAR PDSCH:

- `ra_pdcch_count=264`
- `ra_pdcch_dci_count=0`
- `ra_pdsch_decode_count=0`
- `ra_response_accept_count=0`
- `setting_rar_grant_count=0`

The dedicated RAR candidate trace env reached `result.env`, but no
`PAVONIS_SRSUE_RAR_CANDIDATE_TRACE` rows were printed. The normal RAR trace is
still enough to classify this run: the UE scanned the RA windows and every
`ra_pdcch` row had `n_dl=0`.

This is a geometry/timing miss, not RAR CRC KO and not a RAPID/payload parse
failure. The first transmitted RAR was at gNB slot point `405.8`, while the
UE window opened from `ra_window_start=4017` after PRACH TTI `4014` and printed
opportunities through the end of that window without any `n_dl>0`. The second
transmitted RAR was at gNB `629.8`, while the UE window was anchored at
`6257`. In both cases the RAR lands just beyond the UE-visible response
window after the stage-1 search delay.

The gNB's seven Msg3 CRC KOs are therefore not evidence that the UE decoded
RAR; they are the gNB listening on the Msg3 grants it scheduled after its own
RAR, while the UE trace shows no RAR grant handoff and no Msg3 preparation.

## Judgment

CP2097 answers CP2057 as a timing/geometry miss: RAR is transmitted, but the UE
does not see an RA-PDCCH candidate before its RA response window closes. The
next RF run should not change thresholds, payload, scheduler semantics, or
gateware. First try the cheaper same-envelope point:

- shift `-96..-96`
- CFO `21000..21000 Hz`
- max candidates `3`

Both CP2097 accepted rows used that exact point. Reducing search from `11`
candidates to roughly `3` may move RAR one or more slots earlier and into the
UE response window without a source change. If this still lands late, the next
decision is a source-level timing-reference fix for PRACH/RAR attribution, not
RAR payload work.
