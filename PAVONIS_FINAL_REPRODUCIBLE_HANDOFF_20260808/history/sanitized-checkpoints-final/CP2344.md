`cell_cfg.pdsch.max_ue_mcs=9` cap as the sole change. The sequence log proves

## DownlinkNAS result

The core immediately returns `DownlinkNASTransport`. The gNB schedules its
SRB1 data as 42-byte QPSK PDSCHs, proving the MCS cap is active. The UE trace
shows MCS9 throughout this transport family. The initial slot4814 attempt is
CRC KO at `5.155 dB`; retransmission/new-HARQ activity follows, and slot4861
decodes 42-byte MCS9 CRC OK at `12.647 dB`. The UE then sends an RLC status PDU
with `ack_sn=1`, which the gNB applies to SRB1 DL SN0. Across all traced
42-byte MCS9 attempts, 18 decode CRC OK and 28 CRC KO; the cap substantially
improves the CP2342 MCS28 failure rather than merely changing scheduling.

The bounded UE detail log gives the end-to-end semantic verdict:


Therefore the DownlinkNAS PDSCH, RLC, RRC, and NAS delivery path all work OTA.
No `UplinkNASTransport`, `InitialContextSetup`, or PDU session follows because
the core rejects the presented UE identity. This is no longer an RF/PHY
frontier.

## Verdict and next

CP2344 is a milestone and accepts `max_ue_mcs=9` as the frozen OTA fixture.
The active blocker is qcore subscriber identity/provisioning. Stay no-RF next:
extract the IMSI/SUCI and authentication material configured in the frozen UE,
compare it with qcore's subscriber database and PLMN, and provision or select
a matching subscriber without changing RF, timing, TA, stage1, or scheduler
settings. Then rerun the same frozen shape and judge
`Authentication/Security -> InitialContextSetup -> PDU session -> data`.
