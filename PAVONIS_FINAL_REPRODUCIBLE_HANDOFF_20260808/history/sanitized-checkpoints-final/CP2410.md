# CP2410: RX gain does not recover TX1 PBCH

Date: [date-redacted] Europe/Zurich
RF transmitted: yes, one bounded replay

## Result

CP2410 repeats CP2409B's valid partial-write-safe TX1 CP1166 replay with only
the bladeRF receive gain raised from 36 to 56. Transmit settings, payload,
ports, geometry, helper, and analysis remain unchanged.

RX2 rises from `-37.18` to `-18.01 dBFS` (`+19.17 dB`) and max component rises
from 118 to 979. It remains unclipped. Despite this clean amplitude response,
one-shot decoding is PBCH KO/no CSI and the complete 250 ms sweep remains
`0/847` PBCH CRC OK, `0/847` PBCH PCI1, and `0/847` CSI PCI1. RX1 remains weak
at `-37.61 dBFS`.

Helper health is unchanged: channel1, all `180000000` samples sent in `176130`
writes, zero errors, zero short writes.

## Interpretation

Receiver quantization or insufficient configured bladeRF gain is not the
blocker. Gain56 amplifies the RX2 floor and waveform energy by the expected
amount without improving decoder-visible SNR/coherence. The phone no-cell
result remains a TX1 OTA NR link-budget problem.

The next software-only lever is the phone branch's baseband
`tx_gain_backoff`, currently 10 dB. Source confirms this is PAPR backoff applied
regardless of clipping; clipping is currently disabled. A bounded gNB-only
channel1 bracket should reduce backoff conservatively first (for example 10 to
7 dB, approximately the captured vector's available peak headroom), retain RF
attenuation 5, and use RX2 PBCH/CSI plus peak/clipping as the gate. If that
cannot create decoder-visible SSB without unacceptable clipping, the remaining
remedy is physical antenna/path gain. Keep the phone parked until this bracket
produces a decodable independent downlink.
