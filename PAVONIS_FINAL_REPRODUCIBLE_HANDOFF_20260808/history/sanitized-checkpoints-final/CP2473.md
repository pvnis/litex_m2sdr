# CP2473: Live startup stalls the reader across a full ring

Date: [date-redacted] Europe/Zurich
RF transmitted: yes, bounded private-lab TX2/RX2 diagnostic

## Classification

The startup worker trace, TX-health, and independent RX2 axes are valid. The
phone-monitor axis is void: runtime correctly used the intended 20 ms offset,
but the invocation supplied an obsolete outer override name, so CP2395 expected
its default 30 ms and returned propagation failure after direct process proof.
No phone monitor started. The inner run still completed, stopped, packaged the
startup trace, and passed its TX gates.

## Health and RF

The process records all-timed0, force-continuous0, 20 ms offset, deadline-write1,
and an empty worker arm. The trace begins at handle0 and CP2047 passes all
65,536 full 1022-sample rows with zero short, late, deduplicated, handle-gap, or
ring-slot-mismatch rows. Binary-safe health is zero Soapy timeout,
downlink-late, and release-late lines.

External RX2 repeats the live failure at `-32.50 dBFS`, max675, unclipped,
`0/847` PBCH and `0/847` PCI1 CSI.

## Startup discriminator

The live producer enables the reader on handle0, but hardware count then stays
at 1 through handle256. Publishing those first 257 rows takes 48.042 ms. The
producer then pauses 73.077 ms between handles256 and257, after which hardware
count is 8. Hardware count reaches 256 only at handle490, 143.101 ms after the
first row. The sole timed anchor is nominally 120.820 ms in the future.

The passing CP2470 exact-timed helper behaves differently despite a first
anchor 749.965 ms in the future: hardware count reaches 2 by handle2 in 0.009
ms and reaches 256 by handle480 in 29.155 ms. Thus the discriminator is not
simply anchor lead time or a full ring. It is the live startup's reader/count
progression while the initial timed descriptor is pending.

## Verdict

Late-run descriptor health hid a startup-only state difference. Live wraps the
256-slot publication ring while the reader remains effectively pinned at its
first descriptor, exactly the class of event that can poison subsequent looped
ring order even though later bytes and queue depth are clean.

CP2462 previously showed that a zero-HAS_TIME live run also remained RF-blind,
so do not patch the anchor path yet. Repeat that force-continuous shape with the
startup trace. If the zero-anchor reader still pins through a ring cycle, the
fault is reader-start/loop priming independent of timed gating. If it advances
like CP2471, the startup timed path is causal and CP2462 needs re-interpretation
with direct startup evidence.
