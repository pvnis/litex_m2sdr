# CP2479: Untraced prime128 remains RF-blind and lacks activation proof

## Scope

Repeat CP2478 with zero-copy reader prime128 and the worker trace behind an
untouched arm. The first fresh stamp was consumed by an SSH rc255 before a
usable run; append-only preservation worked and the valid execution used a new
stamp.

## Execution gate

- Valid stamp: `[date-redacted]CP2479_LIVE_ZEROCOPY_PRIME128_NOTRACE_RX2_RF`.
- Phone gNB/process propagation passed for physical channel offset1,
  force-continuous1, all-timed0, zero-copy1, and prime128.
- The worker trace stayed absent as intended.
- TX health was clean: zero Soapy timeout, downlink-late, release-late, and
  final-deficit lines.

## Result

The external RF verdict is negative. RX2 measured `-32.56 dBFS`, max `680`,
with `0/847` PBCH CRC-OK and `0/847` PCI1 CSI windows. RX1 measured
`-42.02 dBFS`, max `203`, also `0/847`. The phone produced 46 rows with no
private PLMN, service, NR cell, or cellular IP.

The mechanism verdict is narrower: this run does not prove prime128 activated.
The environment reached the gNB process, but the unique source log marker
`LitePCIe TX zero-copy reader enabled after priming 128 buffers` is absent.
Only the startup worker and release summaries are present, both before reader
enable. CP2478 emitted the marker and proved handle127 activation; CP2479 did
not. Therefore this is a real RF-negative run, but it is void for claiming that
an untraced active prime128 path itself failed.

## Decision

Do not enlarge the prime. Move the discriminator below the host-visible DMA
boundary: capture the live TX on the M2SDR's own RX after the LitePCIe reader
and gateware/RF path, while retaining only bounded startup telemetry needed to
prove reader activation. A self-RX failure places the fault in the reader/
gateware/TX path; a self-RX pass with bladeRF RX2 failure reopens the external
path/config split.
