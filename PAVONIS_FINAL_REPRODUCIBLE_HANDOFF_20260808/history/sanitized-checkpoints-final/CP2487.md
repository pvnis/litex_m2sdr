## Same-device result

The M2SDR RX2 append began at `[date-redacted]`,
`4.525668647 s` after the first live TX descriptor. It captured exactly
`2,880,000` samples over `5,648` metadata rows with zero RX overflows, chunk
overflows, or timestamp regressions.

The compact 20 ms Pattern-A sweep decoded PCI1 PBCH in `24/24` windows. CSI
SNR ranged from `+6.9` to `+13.1 dB`. The signal remained decoder-coherent
throughout the 250 ms append, several seconds after TX startup.

The 256-row startup worker trace repeats the prime signature: reader enable at
handle/sequence `127`, then two handle jumps totaling 25 skipped handles. It
has zero late or dedup rows. Runtime health is clean: zero Soapy TX timeouts,
zero returned-sample deficit, zero downlink-late events, and zero release-late
events.

## External result and limitation

The bladeRF RX2 capture remains floor-class at `-32.82 dBFS`, max `626`, with
`0/847` PBCH and zero PCI1 CSI windows. However, the helper captures RX1 first
and RX2 second and records only whole-script start/end times. Its RX2 sample
start is therefore not known precisely and is not proven to overlap the
M2SDR append, even though the helper itself started in the same UTC second.

## Verdict

The live channel-1 TX is still decoder-coherent at the M2SDR self-RX tap
`4.526 s` after its first descriptor. This closes a fast late-onset live
baseband/coherence collapse as the explanation for the persistent external
failure. It does not yet distinguish the bladeRF/RF leg from a TX behavior
that differs between local coupling and the external port, because the two
receiver windows were not exactly simultaneous.

The next rung is an RX2-only bladeRF helper with a ready/start-arm handshake,
triggered together with the M2SDR append. That produces a true same-window
split without the current RX1 prelude.
