# CP2691: Channel-1 structured helper passes PBCH on both bladeRF receivers

CP2691 reruns the valid CP2432B shape on the current hardware: the preserved
CP1481 payload is sent through the partial-write-safe CP1166 helper on physical
TX channel 1 at ATT5. Both bladeRF receivers capture at 1842.5 MHz,
11.52 Msps, 10 MHz bandwidth, gain36.

The transmit side is exact and complete: 180,000,000 samples in 176,130 writes,
with zero errors and zero short writes.

## Decode results

RX1:

- one-shot RMS `-44.8555 dBFS`, max61, unclipped;
- one-shot expected PCI1 PBCH CRC OK, CSI `+3.4 dB`;
- aligned sweep `75/847` PBCH and `262/847` CSI, best `+4.2 dB`.

RX2:

- one-shot RMS `-22.9944 dBFS`, max1328, unclipped;
- one-shot expected PCI1 PBCH CRC OK, CSI `+19.6 dB`;
- aligned sweep `489/847` PBCH and `317/847` CSI, best `+21.3 dB`.

The RX1 comparison is especially decisive. CP2689 live RX1 had nearly the same
total level (`-44.8404 dBFS`) but `0/847` PBCH and best CSI only `-3.5 dB`.
The helper therefore gains about 7.7 dB of best-CSI/coherence margin without a
raw-power increase. Channel-1 link budget alone does not explain the live
failure. RX2 also proves that the current antenna-side path can carry a strong
structured NR waveform.

## Verdict

The active defect is specific to live gNB presentation at the external RF
observer, despite clean M2SDR self-RX. Do not change attenuation, gains, phone
policy, MIB, PRACH, scheduler, or NAS.

Next capture one live gNB window simultaneously on bladeRF RX1 and RX2, with
no same-device append and no detector conclusion. If live RX2 decodes, the
remaining fault is receiver/phone-facing coupling and RX1 was the wrong live
observer. If live RX2 also fails while this helper passes, the live runtime
descriptor/cadence path corrupts the RF output in a way the M2SDR self-RX tap
does not expose.
