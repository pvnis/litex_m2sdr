## Result

This is a valid initial-access failure, not the CP2995/CP2996 zero-RACH class.
The phone monitor has 94 rows, including 28 rows exposing the exact NR cell, but
zero in-service and zero private-address rows. The gNB accepts 29 RACH events.
The first 13 RACH procedures produce 64 traced 11-byte PUSCH/Msg3 decode
attempts; every CRC is KO. Their best SINR is only `-7.633 dB`, and the overall
range is `-21.011..-7.633 dB`. The remaining 16 RACH events produce no traced
Msg3 PUSCH. Qcore correctly records zero registration requests, authentication,
registration accepts, or user-plane activations. Both HTTP sides time out
untriggered, so the data axis is void rather than failed.

## CP2992 pass/fail join

The most recent M2SDR attach pass, CP2992, uses the same RX40/ATT16 shape. It
also begins with floor-class Msg3 attempts, but its fourth RACH procedure
transitions to a good PUSCH class: PRACH offset34, 49 PUSCH rows, 14 CRC-OK, and
maximum SINR `+8.814 dB`. Those rows carry the attach forward into qcore and the
private-address state.

CP3005 never makes that transition. Its closest offset control, RACH procedure
12 at offset31, still has five CRC-KO rows and maximum SINR `-12.275 dB`.
Therefore the simple PRACH-delay/TA value does not explain the split. The
current frontier is a run-scale two-state M2SDR uplink presentation problem at
Msg3: the downlink is sufficient for SI, RACH is accepted, and the UE receives
enough RAR to transmit Msg3, but the gNB sees only the floor class.
