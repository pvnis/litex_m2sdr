# Checkpoint 3324: MCS16 hardware-versus-ZMQ uplink cadence audit

Date: [date-redacted]

## Verdict

**PASS (no RF): the remaining MCS16 hardware throughput gap is localized to
hardware-timeline uplink feedback latency and HARQ lifetime.**

The same MCS16 scheduler policy delivers 15.48 Mbit/s uplink over ZMQ but only
3.69 Mbit/s over M2SDR. ZMQ returns a scheduled PUSCH result in a median
3.409 ms and reuses a HARQ process after a median 8 slots. The hardware path
returns the result in 34.850 ms and reuses a HARQ process after a median
39 slots. That delay reduces new-transmission cadence from almost every slot
to a mean 3.51-slot interval and reduces scheduled gross uplink from
16.25 Mbit/s to 3.97 Mbit/s.

This is not a BSR, transport, grant-size, or CPU-decoder ceiling:

- Both paths report only zero-valued short BSR rows during the measured window.
- CP3322 delivers about 93% of its scheduled new-transmission gross rate.
- Hardware PUSCH processing is faster than ZMQ (168.1 us versus 250.3 us
  median), so compute time does not explain the 31.4 ms feedback difference.
- Hardware already grants median 1985-byte transport blocks at MCS16.

The existing 40-slot non-PRACH receive depth is retained. Earlier checkpoints
proved it prevents late hardware samples from losing their assignment; it does
not create the latency. Reducing it would reintroduce discarded slots and
missing HARQ results without making RF samples arrive earlier.

The operator-reported normal bidirectional throughput on a USB LiteX SDR is
recorded only as external context. It was not produced by this campaign and is
not used as checkpoint evidence.

## Experiment

Two no-RF ZMQ runs reused the pinned CP3316 MCS16 binary, scheduler profile, UE
role, and sustained 45-second bidirectional traffic shape:

1. An info-level control reproduced 7.50/15.06 Mbit/s DL/UL.
2. A debug-level diagnostic changed only the gNB log level and reproduced
   7.60/15.48 Mbit/s DL/UL.

The debug run emitted scheduler and PUSCH records needed for a like-for-like
comparison with CP3322. The 948 MB remote log was not copied or read wholesale.
A compact parser processed it on [host-redacted] and emitted only aggregate cadence,
HARQ, result-latency, CRC, processing-time, and BSR statistics.

The parser's scheduler-to-result join uses FIFO matching by HARQ process and
transport-block size. A direct bounded row check also showed the same slot
label at the scheduler and PHY result boundaries separated by about 35 ms on
hardware, consistent with the aggregate.

## Interpretation

The hardware scheduler has demand and can allocate large MCS16 grants, but
each transmission remains outstanding while the shifted hardware receive
timeline catches up. The ZMQ path cycles eight HARQ processes at normal
one-slot cadence; hardware consumes all sixteen available process identifiers
and still waits about 39 slots before reuse. The scheduled-rate ratio
(4.09x) closely matches the delivered-rate ratio (4.19x).

The next change must address the timeline at its source. Before RF, trace the
exact relationship among the OCUDU TX timestamp, the M2SDR Soapy TX lead,
actual RF emission, the RX hardware timestamp, the dynamic non-PRACH offset,
and the reported scheduler slot. Do not [interface-redacted] BSR, grant size, MCS, or receive
ring depth again on this evidence.
