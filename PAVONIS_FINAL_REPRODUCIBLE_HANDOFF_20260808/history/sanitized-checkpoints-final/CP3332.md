# CP3332: 10 ms MCS16 OTA valid partial

## Verdict

**VALID PARTIAL.** The corrected 10 ms shape passed its pre-UE gates, acquired
the cell, reached the private data path, and sustained **3.903533 Mbit/s DL**.
It did not deliver a valid uplink throughput result: the UE attempted
`437296` bytes, the core received `0`, and no receiver acknowledgement
returned.

The run nevertheless proves one mechanism. Median scheduler-to-PUSCH-result
latency fell from CP3322's `34.850 ms` to `24.897 ms`, almost exactly the
`10 ms` physical-lead reduction. It also exposes the next blocker: periodic
deadline-write sample abandonment coincided with a frame-scale uplink
coherence collapse.

## Evidence

- Outer expected lead and live readback both reported `10000000 ns`.
- Cell selection passed at `12.804 dB`, with CFO `-4094.981 Hz`.
- The gNB encoded one RAR and the established preconnected traffic endpoints
  ran for the requested `45 s` per direction.
- During the healthy interval `[date-redacted]` through `[date-redacted]`, PUSCH was
  `2250/2383` CRC OK (`94.42%`) with median accepted SINR `21.3 dB`.
- At `[date-redacted]`, PUSCH changed from the roughly `20-25 dB` class to
  `9/137` CRC OK; subsequent visible rows were all CRC KO around
  `-24` to `-26 dB`.
- The TX stop summary reports `12` pre-write deadline breaks, zero
  deadline-controlled Soapy write timeouts, and `85096` abandoned/deficit
  samples. Generic Soapy-timeout and downlink-late log counts are also zero.
- Deadline-break transitions occur at logical blocks `98500`, `121500`,
  `144500`, `190100`, `235700`, and `419500`. The `121500` transition maps
  to the same second as the PUSCH cliff.

The compact cadence parser selected the preceding downlink phase because that
was the densest healthy PUSCH window. Its `0.192 Mbit/s` scheduled gross rate
there is reverse traffic during DL, not the sustained UL workload. The
explicit UL-phase mirror contains only about four seconds before its row cap,
so it is useful for the cliff signature but not for a 45-second scheduler-rate
claim.

## Interpretation

The 10 ms lead is causally useful but not yet operationally safe under the full
ladder load. The uplink failure is not evidence that MCS16, BSR, or the
scheduler regressed: the same run carried thousands of high-SINR PUSCHs before
the deadline-loss event. It is a time-local TX/timeline failure that begins
exactly when the sustained uplink workload starts.

The break clusters are separated by approximately `23 s`, matching the
periodicity already visible in CP3328's 5 ms gate. That is a strong lead for
the next no-RF forensic checkpoint, but CP3332 alone does not prove a DMA-ring
wrap defect.

Do not rerun this shape unchanged. Determine the periodic loss mechanism,
apply a narrowly gated fix if the source evidence supports one, and require a
clean bounded gNB-only gate before another OTA attempt.
