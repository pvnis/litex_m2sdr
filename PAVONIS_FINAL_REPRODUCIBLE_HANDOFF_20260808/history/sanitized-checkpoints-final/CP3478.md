# Checkpoint 3478: MCS9/MCS10 repeat confirms the downlink split

## Verdict

**PASS: the same-run MCS9 advantage reproduced strongly, with valid endpoints
and strict-clean TX delivery.** CP3477 was not run-scale scatter. The campaign
now has a stable modulation-boundary result, but it has not yet separated the
known software-decoder sensitivity difference from an additional hardware-path
impairment.

## Repeat result

| Population | MCS9 | MCS10 | MCS9/MCS10 yield ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| All RV0 | 64/73 (87.67%) | 38/78 (48.72%) | 1.80x |
| 52 PRB / 1089-byte RV0 | 51/53 (96.23%) | 25/58 (43.10%) | 2.23x |

The all-RV0 two-sided Fisher result is `p=2.72e-7`; the equal-geometry result is
`p=3.86e-10`. Median reported SNR is effectively matched: 3.642 dB at MCS9 and
3.721 dB at MCS10.

Pooled over CP3477 and CP3478, all-RV0 yield is 85/132 (64.39%) at MCS9 versus
50/141 (35.46%) at MCS10 (`p=2.09e-6`). The pooled equal-geometry result is
63/88 (71.59%) versus 31/97 (31.96%, `p=8.37e-8`). Both runs therefore point
in the same direction under an interleaved, equal-load design.

## Interpretation

MCS9 and MCS10 have almost equal nominal spectral efficiency but cross from
QPSK to 16QAM. The hardware path therefore has a repeatable robustness gap at
that transition. CP3472 also measured a 1.5 dB MCS10 penalty in the exact
legacy software decoder under AWGN, so the repeat does not by itself prove an
extra M2SDR defect.

The next checkpoint stays no-RF and compares CRC outcome against reported SNR
for both hardware runs and the CP3472 AWGN curves. If the observed split is
explained by the decoder curves, the old "M2SDR 16QAM defect" theory closes as
an operating-margin effect. If MCS10 remains materially worse after that
conditioning, the next gate must localize the residual with transmitter-
referenced symbols. Bandwidth, MIMO, and producer-stall headroom remain locked.
